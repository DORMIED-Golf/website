Thirteen Ball Metrics, No Club Data: Foresight Revives the GC2 Name at $1,699

Foresight Sports revives the GC2 name on a $1,699 dual-camera launch monitor shipping in October. What the spec sheet leaves out, and why the price matters.

By Travis R.  ·  October 1, 2026  ·  Launch Monitors  ·  4 min read

Image: The Golf Wire

What Happened With Foresight Sports?

Foresight Sports has launched a new GC2 dual-camera launch monitor at $1,699, with preorders open now and shipping October 9 to 15. It measures thirteen ball metrics and no club data, includes a 30-day Foresight+ trial, and reuses a badge first introduced in 2012 to enter golf's most crowded launch monitor price band.

Foresight Sports has brought back the GC2 badge, attached it to a new dual-camera launch monitor, and priced it at $1,699. Preorders opened this week at foresightsports.com, as a standalone unit or bundled into a SIM IN A BOX Net package, with shipping scheduled between October 9 and 15. Buyers get a 30-day trial of Foresight+, the software layer the company updated earlier this year alongside Premier.

The name is not an accident. The original GC2 arrived in 2012 and did more to normalize photometric launch monitors in club fitting bays than any product before it. It cost multiples of this unit, it still trades briskly on the used market more than a decade later, and it is the reason a generation of fitters stopped trusting radar indoors. Reusing that badge on an entry-level box is a deliberate withdrawal from the brand equity account.

Why Did Foresight Revive the GC2 Name Instead of Launching a New One?

Because the alternative was launching an unknown product into the single most crowded price band in golf technology. Between roughly $700 and $3,000 sit Rapsodo's MLM2PRO, Garmin's R10, SkyTrak+, and Bushnell's Launch Pro, which is itself built on Foresight camera technology. A new name in that bracket is a marketing problem. A name that fitters already associate with measured ball data is a shortcut.

The positioning language in the announcement is unusually direct about the target. Radar systems sit behind the golfer and estimate impact through algorithms; the GC2's two high-speed cameras sit beside the ball and measure it. That is a real engineering distinction, not a dressed-up claim, and it is the argument Foresight has made since 2012. The difference now is that the company is making it to a buyer who was never going to spend GCQuad money.

What Does the GC2 Leave Out at $1,699?

The spec sheet lists thirteen ball metrics: carry, total distance, ball speed, launch angle, launch direction, back spin, side spin, total spin, spin axis, offline, peak height, descent angle and hang time. It lists no club data. No head speed, no angle of attack, no path, no face angle. That omission is how $1,699 happens, and it is worth understanding before anyone compares this to a GCQuad on accuracy alone.

For a golfer building a garage sim or validating gapping at the range, ball-only data is sufficient and always has been. For a coach diagnosing a strike pattern or a fitter dialing a shaft, it is not. Foresight has drawn the line in roughly the same place it drew it with the original GC2, which needed the separate HMT attachment to read the club. The company is being consistent. It is just being consistent at a price that invites a different comparison set.

How Does the GC2 Fit Into the Revelyst Golf Technology Portfolio?

Awkwardly, on paper. Revelyst Golf Technology owns Foresight, Bushnell Golf, GolfLogix and PinSeeker, which means the GC2 is now competing for the same consumer who was being routed to Bushnell's Launch Pro. Internal overlap of that kind is usually a decision rather than an oversight, and the decision here looks like a bet that the Foresight name converts better at retail than the Bushnell one does in this category.

The other tell is Foresight+. Hardware margin at $1,699 is thinner than hardware margin at $17,000, and the 30-day trial is the funnel. This is the subscription model arriving in launch monitors properly, the way it arrived in GPS and then in simulation software. Buy the box once, pay for the ecosystem annually. Foresight's visibility has been flat month over month at 114th of 215 brands tracked, which is low for a company whose hardware sits in nearly every serious fitting bay in the country. Volume products fix that number. They also change who the brand belongs to.

Watch what happens to Launch Pro pricing and to Foresight+ attach rates over the next two quarters. If the GC2 sells and the software converts, Revelyst has found a repeatable way to monetize a premium name downmarket. If it sells and the software does not, the company will have traded fifteen years of fitter credibility for a one-time hardware transaction at Rapsodo's price point. The October ship window is the first data.

What Else Do Golfers Ask About Foresight Sports?

How much does the new Foresight Sports GC2 cost and when does it ship?

The GC2 is priced at $1,699 and preorders are open at foresightsports.com, either as a standalone unit or inside a SIM IN A BOX Net package. Units are scheduled to ship between October 9 and 15.

Does the Foresight GC2 measure club data?

No. The published spec sheet lists thirteen ball metrics including ball speed, launch angle, spin axis and descent angle, but no club head speed, angle of attack, path or face angle. That omission is a large part of how the $1,699 price is reached.

How is the GC2 different from radar launch monitors?

Radar units sit behind the golfer and use algorithms to estimate what happened at impact. The GC2 uses two high-speed cameras positioned beside the ball to measure impact directly. It is the same argument Foresight has made since the original GC2 in 2012.

Does the GC2 compete with Bushnell Launch Pro?

It overlaps with it. Revelyst Golf Technology owns both Foresight Sports and Bushnell Golf, and the Launch Pro is built on Foresight camera technology, so the GC2 targets a consumer the portfolio was already serving under a different badge.

What is Foresight+ and is it included with the GC2?

Foresight+ is the company's software layer, updated this year alongside Premier to connect the Foresight hardware ecosystem. GC2 buyers receive a 30-day free trial, which functions as the entry point to a recurring software relationship rather than a permanent inclusion.
