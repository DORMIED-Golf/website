One Player Built JumboMax's Entire Brand. He Doesn't Play the PGA Tour Anymore.

JumboMax has signed PGA Tour Americas rookie Paul Chang to play its JMX UltraLite grips, a cheap fix for a brand built almost entirely on Bryson DeChambeau.

By James K.  ·  September 23, 2026  ·  Grips  ·  3 min read

Image: The Golf Wire

What Happened With JumboMax?

JumboMax has signed Paul Chang, a former Virginia standout and No. 13 in the World Amateur Golf Rankings, to play JMX UltraLite grips on PGA Tour Americas. Chang made four straight cuts to open his pro career. The deal is a low-cost pipeline play for a brand whose visibility has rested almost entirely on Bryson DeChambeau since 2020.

JumboMax has signed Paul Chang, the former Virginia standout who reached No. 13 in the World Amateur Golf Rankings, to play its JMX UltraLite grips through his rookie season on PGA Tour Americas. Chang made the cut in each of his first four professional starts and won twice at Virginia in 2025-26. On paper it is a routine developmental-tour ambassador deal. In context it is a grip company quietly addressing the fact that its most famous endorser plays golf almost nobody watches on network television anymore.

Grips are the strangest category in the equipment business. They are the cheapest thing in the bag, the only component every single golfer touches on every single shot, and the one nobody replaces until the surface has gone shiny and slick. Brand preference gets built almost entirely through visibility: a player wins, someone screengrabs the WITB, and a $12 part sells. JumboMax has never had a distribution problem or a product problem. It has a visibility problem, and it has had one since 2022.

Why Does JumboMax Need Players Beyond Bryson DeChambeau?

The brand's entire cultural footprint traces back to one pair of hands. Bryson DeChambeau won the 2020 U.S. Open with JumboMax grips on a set of single-length irons and the oversized grip went from orthopedic curiosity to a legitimate fitting conversation overnight. That was a once-a-decade marketing event for a small manufacturer. It was also, in retrospect, a single point of failure. DeChambeau's move to LIV removed JumboMax from the broadcast windows where equipment curiosity actually converts, and Jason Day, the other marquee name on the roster, is not a player anyone associates with grip size.

Signing a PGA Tour Americas rookie is the inexpensive correction. Developmental tour deals cost a fraction of a PGA Tour contract and the math is asymmetric: most of these players never break through, but the ones who do arrive on the big stage already gripped, already familiar with the fitting, already on the record about it. Titleist and Ping have run this pipeline model for decades. A grip company doing it is less common, largely because grip companies historically bought presence through free installation at tour vans rather than named ambassadors. JumboMax is buying a name instead, which suggests it understands that the product needs an advocate, not just availability.

What Does a 25 Percent Lighter Grip Actually Change?

The UltraLite spec is the part serious fitters should care about. A grip that is up to 25 percent lighter than standard is not a comfort claim, it is a swingweight claim. Pull meaningful mass out of the butt end of a club and the head feels heavier through the swing, which is why lightweight grips have quietly become a build tool rather than a preference. Combine that with an oversized profile and you get the thing JumboMax has always argued: a larger, lighter grip reduces hand action without adding static weight, which is the trade-off that killed most oversized grips before this one.

That is a genuinely defensible engineering position, and it is also a hard sell, because oversized grips look wrong to people who grew up on standard round rubber. Conversion happens by demonstration, not by copy. Which is exactly why the brand's current standing, a slide of more than 18 percent in a month and a position around the middle of the Bagsand Accessories field on the DORMIED Index, reads as a mindshare result rather than a product one.

Is a Developmental Tour Deal Enough to Rebuild Mindshare?

One rookie will not fix it. Four cuts in four starts is a promising month, not a career. But Chang is the kind of signing that only works if there are five or six more like it, spread across the Korn Ferry and Americas fields, waiting for two or three to graduate.

The alternative is waiting for another DeChambeau, and brands that wait for lightning usually just wait. JumboMax has the better argument in the fitting bay than it does on television, and this deal is the first sign it plans to stop relying on one player to make that argument for it.

What Else Do Golfers Ask About JumboMax?

Who is Paul Chang and why did JumboMax sign him?

Chang is a former University of Virginia player who reached No. 13 in the World Amateur Golf Rankings and won twice in 2025-26. He made the cut in each of his first four professional starts and will play JumboMax JMX UltraLite grips on PGA Tour Americas.

What does the JMX UltraLite grip actually do?

It is up to 25 percent lighter than a standard grip. Removing mass from the butt end of a club raises swingweight, so the head feels heavier through the swing, which is what lets JumboMax offer an oversized profile without the static weight penalty that sank earlier oversized grips.

Why is JumboMax's reliance on Bryson DeChambeau a problem?

DeChambeau's 2020 U.S. Open win put oversized grips into the mainstream fitting conversation, but his move to LIV removed JumboMax from the PGA Tour broadcast windows where equipment curiosity converts into sales. The brand's cultural footprint has rested on one player since.

Why sign a developmental tour player instead of a PGA Tour name?

Developmental tour deals cost a fraction of a PGA Tour contract. Most signees never break through, but those who do arrive on the main tour already fitted and already on the record about the product. It is the pipeline model Titleist and Ping have run for decades.
