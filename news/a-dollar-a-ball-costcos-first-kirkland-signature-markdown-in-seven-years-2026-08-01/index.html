A Dollar a Ball: Costco's First Kirkland Signature Markdown in Seven Years Isn't a Promotion

Costco just discounted Kirkland Signature golf balls to $1 each, the first markdown in seven years. Why it's an inventory signal, not a promotion.

By Adam R.  ·  August 1, 2026  ·  Balls  ·  3 min read

Image: MyGolfSpy

What Happened With Kirkland Golf?

Costco is selling the Kirkland Signature golf ball at $23.99 for two dozen, roughly $6 off, its first markdown on the SKU in seven years according to a warehouse employee who tipped MyGolfSpy. Some stores started early; others run Saturday windows expiring at 1 p.m. The cost-plus pricing suggests inventory clearing before a new spec, not a promotion.

Seven years of no discounts, and then a weekend sale with posted hours. Costco is selling the Kirkland Signature golf ball at $23.99 for two dozen, roughly $6 off, and according to a warehouse employee who tipped MyGolfSpy, the chain has never run a single promotion on the SKU in his time there. Some locations reportedly started early. Others are running Saturday morning windows that expire at 1 p.m.

Why Did Costco Discount the Kirkland Signature Golf Balls?

The price is not the story. A dollar a ball is remarkable in the way that Kirkland has always been remarkable, which is to say it was already remarkable at $30. The story is that Costco discounted anything at all. The Kirkland Signature model is cost-plus by design, with a hard internal markup ceiling that most retailers would consider a rounding error. The margin is in the membership fee, not the shelf. When a retailer already prices near the floor takes another six dollars off, the money is not coming from a marketing budget. It is coming from inventory that needs to move.

Which raises the more interesting question: move ahead of what. The Kirkland ball has a documented history of quiet supplier and spec changes, from the original four-piece run through the Nassau-produced versions to the current three-piece urethane build out of Asia. Each transition has been announced by golfers on forums before it was announced by Costco, because Costco does not announce anything. A clearance-shaped markdown on a product with no seasonal cycle and no competing SKU beside it on the pallet looks less like a customer-acquisition play and more like the warehouse making room. Watch the box art and the compression numbers in the fall.

Which Brands Does the Costco Golf Ball Sale Pressure?

The brand that should be uncomfortable here is not Titleist. Pro V1 buyers are not cross-shopping a pallet, and Acushnet has never lost meaningful volume to Kirkland at the top of the funnel. The pressure lands on the DTC tier: Vice, Snell, Seed, Cut, OnCore, every brand whose entire pitch is tour-caliber urethane at $30 to $40 a dozen without the Titleist tax. Those companies need somewhere around $2 a ball to cover customer acquisition costs, fulfillment, and the inevitable discount code. Kirkland at $1 with no shipping, no email capture, and no funnel does not compete on the same axis. It removes the axis. A DTC ball brand can out-market Kirkland easily, because Kirkland does no marketing. What it cannot do is out-price a product that exists to justify a $65 annual membership.

That absence of marketing is why Kirkland sits at 133rd of 215 on our brand index with a score barely above a single point, a number that measures visibility and cultural presence rather than units sold. The gap between those two things is the whole point of the brand. There is no tour seeding program, no Instagram account, no ambassador, no launch event, no press release. The product's entire promotional apparatus is a stack on a concrete floor next to the rotisserie chickens and a MyGolfSpy ball test that keeps saying it has improved in spin consistency and predictability without ever claiming it is a Pro V1. That last part matters. The honest positioning is what has kept the ball credible through three generations of spec drift.

How Would Repeat Markdowns Change the Golf Ball Value Tier?

The thing to watch is whether this becomes normal. If Costco starts running periodic Kirkland ball promotions the way it does with tires and hearing aids, the brand shifts from a fixed price anchor to a promotional one, and the entire value tier has to reprice underneath it. If it stays a one-off, it was inventory clearing before a new build, and the next version will show up unannounced with a slightly different core and a forum thread six hours later. Either outcome tells you something. Kirkland remains the only brand in golf that moves the market without ever saying a word about it.
