Wide Sizes Cost Shelf Space Nobody Has. Under Armour Engineered Around Them.

Under Armour's Drive Pro Clone SL solves golf's wide-foot problem without width SKUs. Why that's an inventory decision, not a comfort feature.

By Adam R.  ·  August 26, 2026  ·  Performance  ·  3 min read

Image: MyGolfSpy

What Happened

Under Armour's Drive Pro Clone SL was named one of the best golf shoes for wide feet, alongside the FootJoy Pro/SL and the $110 Skechers Go Golf Flow SL. Unlike the other two, it offers no wide sizing. Its adaptive Clone upper and HOVR cushioning fit both narrow and wide feet, though traction rates below average.

Golf's wide-fit problem was never really a design problem. It is an inventory problem, and only one brand in the category has the retail footprint to solve it the expensive way.

That context matters for where the Under Armour Drive Pro Clone SL just landed. MyGolfSpy's roundup of shoes that actually work for wide feet named three: the FootJoy Pro/SL, the Skechers Go Golf Flow SL at $110, and the Drive Pro Clone SL. Two of the three get there by stocking width options. The Under Armour does not. One tester summed up the difference: "Clone is designed to mold to your foot, and that's exactly what this shoe does. Narrow, wide, it doesn't matter."

Run the math on what the other approach costs. A men's shoe carried from size 7 through 15 is already nine to seventeen SKUs depending on half sizes. Add a wide and you double it. Add an extra-wide, which FootJoy does on the Pro/SL, and you are carrying three parallel inventories of the same shoe, in every colorway, in every account. FootJoy can absorb that because it owns the green-grass wall and has spent decades training pro shops to fit width the way a shoe store does. Under Armour Golf does not have that wall, and buying its way onto it would cost more than the shoe program earns.

So the Clone upper is a merchandising decision wearing a materials story. The adaptive knit stretches laterally across the forefoot instead of asking the buyer to self-diagnose a width they may not know, layered over HOVR cushioning that testers rated well for both comfort and lateral stability. That combination is unusual: most foam packages that feel good on a walking round go soft under a real weight shift. The traction is the honest weakness here, rated below average, which is a real limitation in wet conditions and a fixable one on the next tooling cycle.

The part the industry should be paying attention to is what a one-width shoe does to online returns. Wide-footed buyers are the highest-return cohort in footwear, and golf shoes sold direct carry return rates that quietly eat the margin advantage DTC is supposed to deliver. Buying golf shoes online with wide feet is like ordering a suit by mail and hoping the tailor guessed. Every pair that ships back costs freight both directions plus the markdown on a used-looking box. A single-width shoe that genuinely fits a broad range of foot shapes is not a comfort feature. It is a working capital feature, and it is the reason brands without pro shop distribution keep gravitating toward knit and adaptive uppers while FootJoy keeps stocking three widths.

None of which is currently doing much for Under Armour Golf's standing. The brand sits 76th of 215 in the DORMIED Index, down 18.2 percent month over month, which is what it looks like when a division makes competent product and generates almost no conversation around it. Under Armour's golf footwear has been quietly decent for several product cycles while the cultural oxygen went to Malbon, Sun Day Red and whatever Nike does next. A tester quote in a fit roundup is the kind of win that moves units at Golf Galaxy and moves nothing on a timeline.

What to watch is whether Under Armour treats Clone as a shoe or as a platform. If the construction migrates across the golf footwear line, the brand has a genuine category position nobody else is claiming: the shoe you buy when you have given up on width charts. If it stays a single model with soft traction and no marketing behind the fit story, it will keep showing up in roundups written by people who tested it, and nowhere else.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does the Under Armour Drive Pro Clone SL come in a wide size?

No. The shoe uses a single width. The Clone upper is built to stretch and mold around the forefoot, which is why testers reported it fitting both narrow and wide feet without a dedicated wide SKU.

What is the weakness of the Drive Pro Clone SL?

Traction. It was rated below average, which matters most in wet conditions. Comfort and stability from the HOVR cushioning both rated well.

Why do most golf brands avoid stocking wide sizes?

Cost. A men's run from size 7 to 15 is already nine to seventeen SKUs. Adding a wide doubles it and adding an extra-wide triples it, across every colorway and every account. FootJoy can carry that inventory because of its green-grass distribution.

How does fit affect direct-to-consumer footwear margins?

Wide-footed buyers return online footwear at high rates, and each return costs freight both ways plus markdown on the box. A shoe that fits a broad range of foot shapes reduces returns, which is a working capital benefit rather than just a comfort one.

Where does Under Armour Golf currently rank?

76th of 215 brands in the DORMIED Index, down 18.2 percent month over month, despite several cycles of competent footwear product.
