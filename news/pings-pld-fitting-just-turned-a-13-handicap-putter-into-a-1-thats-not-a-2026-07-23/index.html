PING's PLD Fitting Just Turned a 13-Handicap Putter Into a 1. That's Not a Marketing Number.

PING's PLD Custom putter fitting cut a MyGolfSpy tester's PuttView handicap from 13 to 1. The result reframes the case for buying any premium putter off the rack.

By Travis R.  ·  July 23, 2026  ·  Clubs  ·  3 min read

Image: MyGolfSpy

A twelve-stroke swing in PuttView handicap is not the kind of result a putter fitting is supposed to produce. It is the kind of result that either validates the entire premise of custom putter fitting or exposes how badly most golfers have been served by off-the-rack putters for the last thirty years. Both can be true.

MyGolfSpy's write-up of a PING PLD fitting lands on that first conclusion. The tester's iPING session, the standard opening move in a PLD fit, flagged heavy forward press, significant deloft, and a slight arc. PING's master fitter built around the stroke rather than trying to correct it. Head style, hosel, loft, and lie were dialed to the stroke's actual mechanics. The finished putter cut the tester's PuttView handicap from 13 to 1 and eliminated a persistent right miss.

The iPING app has been around since 2012, which makes it one of the longer-running data collection tools in consumer golf. PING has spent more than a decade quietly building a database of amateur putting strokes while the rest of the industry chased face inserts and counterbalancing. That patience is the story here. The PLD Custom program, launched in earnest in 2022, is the retail expression of infrastructure PING has been assembling since the Karsten Manufacturing days. Scotty Cameron's Tour Studio does something similar at the top end. No one else in the category has this kind of vertical stack.

The number worth sitting with is the five-stroke spread across the entire mallet field in MyGolfSpy's Most Wanted testing. Best to worst, off the rack, top to bottom of the category, five strokes. A fitted PLD produced a twelve-stroke gap against the same tester's gamer. If those numbers hold up across a broader sample, the argument for buying any premium putter without a fitting starts to look indefensible. That is a problem for every retailer selling $500 putters off a wall rack, and a tailwind for the small handful of brands with genuine fitting infrastructure.

PING's global positioning in DORMIED's tracking has been stable rather than climbing, which fits the brand's posture. PING does not chase news cycles. It does not do Times Square. It does not run PXG-style fitting studios in every metro. It builds product, sells through fitters, and lets the results do the talking. That approach reads as sleepy in a category dominated by launch calendars and tour WITB screenshots. It also produces articles like this one, where an independent tester walks away with data suggesting the fitting was worth every dollar.

The question PING has to answer over the next 24 months is whether PLD Custom scales without diluting. The two-month wait time the tester experienced is a supply constraint, not a marketing strategy. Scotty Cameron has managed similar demand for two decades by leaning into scarcity. LAB Golf is trying to solve for volume right now and finding out how hard it is. PING sits somewhere in between, with the manufacturing base to grow and the brand discipline to not overextend. Watch the wait times. If they hold at eight weeks a year from now, PING has found the ceiling. If they stretch, the category has shifted underneath everyone else.
