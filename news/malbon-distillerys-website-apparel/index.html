A Distillery's Website Is Now an Apparel Channel: Malbon and Bushmills Found the Angle

Malbon and Bushmills launched a 12-piece collection today. The real story is the distillery's website becoming a non-endemic golf apparel channel.

By James K.  ·  September 16, 2026  ·  Trendy/Lifestyle  ·  3 min read

Image: MyGolfSpy

What Happened With Malbon?

Malbon and Bushmills released a 12-piece apparel collection today at Malbon stores, malbongolf.com and bushmills.com. Designs pull from Bushmills' vintage advertising archive rather than current branding: heritage plaid, tailored cuts, earth-toned separates. The strategic value is distribution. Bushmills' direct-to-consumer site puts Malbon in front of premium spirits buyers who never visit golf websites.

Spirits brands have been buying their way into golf for two decades, and it almost always looks the same: signage on a par 3, a branded tent behind the 17th, a bottle in the winner's hands. Bushmills went a different route and put its 400-year archive on a golf shirt instead.

The collection landed today: 12 pieces built with Malbon, heritage plaid, tailored cuts, earth-toned separates, with the design cues pulled from Bushmills' vintage advertising archive rather than anything currently on a bottle. It is available at Malbon stores, malbongolf.com, and bushmills.com. That last one is the part worth paying attention to.

Why Is Malbon Collaborating With an Irish Whiskey Brand?

Malbon's collaboration résumé has skewed hard toward footwear and streetwear, partners who speak the same language and sell to the same 28-year-old who found golf through a YouTube channel. Bushmills speaks none of that. It is a distillery older than most countries, and the only thing it shares with Malbon is a customer who has disposable income and a Saturday to spend.

Which, as demographic overlap goes, is close to perfect. Premium spirits and golf have been chasing the same wallet for years without doing much together beyond hospitality. The interesting choice here is restraint. Working from the old advertising art instead of the current logo lockup means the pieces read as heritage apparel that happens to reference whiskey, not as merch. The difference sounds small on a mood board and it is enormous on a chest pocket.

What Does Selling on Bushmills.com Do for Malbon?

This is the actual business news. A 12-piece capsule is not a revenue event for either company. Twelve pieces is low inventory risk, fast sell-through, and a campaign's worth of content for a fraction of what a full seasonal line costs to develop. Nobody is building a quarter around it.

What Bushmills brings is a marketing budget on a scale apparel margins cannot generate, and a direct-to-consumer storefront full of people who have never clicked on a golf website. Non-endemic distribution is the hardest thing in this category to buy. Malbon can spend on paid social and reach golfers it already reaches. It cannot easily reach a 45-year-old buying a bottle for a gift and finding a quarter-zip in the same cart. Spirits companies have the audience and the co-marketing money. Golf apparel brands have the product and the cultural momentum. That trade is going to get copied.

Can Malbon Keep Its Collab Cadence Going?

The strategic risk is obvious enough. A brand that grows on collaborations eventually becomes a surface other brands rent, and the identity thins out with every drop. Malbon has been better than most at avoiding this, largely because it art-directs the partner rather than the reverse, and the Bushmills work holds that line. But the cadence has to keep producing ideas, not just logos in proximity.

The timing suggests it knows that. Malbon sits inside the global top ten of 215 brands on the DORMIED Index, which is rarefied air for a company this young, but its score slipped 18.5 percent month over month in August. Attention brands live and die in the gap between drops, and a heritage partner with no golf footprint is a smarter way to fill one than a fifth colorway of something familiar.

Watch what comes next, because it will tell you whether this was a direction or a novelty. If the next partner is another old-world house with an archive and a DTC site, Malbon has quietly found a repeatable model: borrow someone else's history, borrow their customer list, keep the design control. If the next one is a sneaker, this was a fun capsule and nothing more. Either way, whiskey-branded golf apparel is not a crowded category today, and it will be by next spring.

What Else Do Golfers Ask About Malbon?

Where can you buy the Malbon x Bushmills collection?

It is available today at Malbon stores, malbongolf.com and bushmills.com. The distillery's own storefront is the unusual channel here, since it reaches spirits buyers rather than golfers.

How many pieces are in the Malbon Bushmills collaboration?

Twelve. It is a capsule, not a seasonal line, which means low inventory risk and fast sell-through for both parties.

What does the collection actually look like?

Heritage plaid, tailored cuts and earth-toned separates. The design references come from Bushmills' vintage advertising archive rather than the brand's current logo, so the pieces read as heritage apparel rather than merch.

Why would a whiskey brand fund a golf apparel collaboration?

Premium spirits and golf chase the same customer, and spirits marketing budgets are far larger than apparel margins can generate. Bushmills supplies reach and a non-endemic storefront; Malbon supplies the product and the design control.
