Ten Years at Winged Foot, Now on the Vendor Side: Johnnie-O's Quietest Advantage

Johnnie-O hired 2023 PGA Merchandiser of the Year Katie Wiedmar from Winged Foot to lead green grass sales. Why the buyer-to-vendor pipeline is the real war.

By Adam R.  ·  August 12, 2026  ·  Trendy/Lifestyle  ·  3 min read

Image: The Golf Wire

What Happened With Johnnie-O?

Johnnie-O's National Sales Manager for Green Grass Sales, Katie Wiedmar, appears on the Association of Golf Merchandisers podcast The Fairway to Retail Success. Wiedmar spent over a decade at Winged Foot, prepared its shop for the 2020 U.S. Open, and won 2023 PGA Merchandiser of the Year in the Private category. The hire targets golf's last full-margin channel.

The most valuable hire in green grass apparel is almost never a salesperson. It is a buyer, preferably one who spent a decade deciding which vendors got wall space and which got the back corner near the hats.

Who Is Katie Wiedmar, Johnnie-O's Green Grass Lead?

Katie Wiedmar is that hire. Johnnie-O's National Sales Manager for Green Grass Sales, and the 2023 PGA Merchandiser of the Year in the Private category, appears on the latest episode of The Fairway to Retail Success, the Association of Golf Merchandisers podcast, to talk through her move from the retail side to the vendor side. Before Johnnie-O, she spent more than ten years at Winged Foot, including the run-up to the 2020 U.S. Open, which is the merchandising equivalent of building an entire seasonal business inside a fourteen-month window and having exactly one chance to get the sizing curve right.

Green grass is the last channel in golf apparel that behaves the way brands wish every channel behaved. No marketplace leakage, no third-party sellers undercutting MAP, no algorithmic race to the bottom on a Tuesday in February. Full margin, member accounts, and a buyer who will happily reorder the same navy quarter-zip for four straight years if it moves. The catch is that access is rationed. A private club shop carries maybe eight to twelve apparel vendors, the rack space is finite, and the open-to-buy is set months before anyone sees a line sheet. You do not break in with a good product. You break in with a relationship and a delivery date the buyer believes.

Why Are Golf Apparel Brands Hiring Green Grass Buyers?

Which is why the buyer-to-vendor pipeline has quietly become the recruiting strategy across the category. Peter Millar, Holderness and Bourne, Greyson and Fairway and Greene are all fishing in the same pond, and the pond is small: AGM counts roughly 900 buyers against 200 vendors. Hiring someone who has sat on the buying side means hiring someone who understands why a shop cancels a pre-book in March, what a championship year does to a shop's cash position, and how much a poorly timed shipment costs in trust. That knowledge does not exist in a general apparel sales resume. It exists in about a thousand people worldwide, and the brands know exactly who they are.

For Johnnie-O specifically, this is the correct pressure point. The brand came out of Laguna Beach in 2005 selling a prep-meets-surf hybrid that reads as Southern California by way of a Connecticut lacrosse camp, and it grew on specialty retail before golf became its center of gravity. The product is competent: solid stretch woven shorts, the four-way performance polos, a fit that flatters a wider range of bodies than the athletic-cut brands admit. But the product is not the differentiator. Nobody at a member-guest is identifying a Johnnie-O polo by its collar construction from twenty feet. The differentiator is being on the rack when a member walks in and needs something before the shotgun start. That is a distribution war, and distribution wars are won by people, not by fabric.

How Does the Golf Merchandisers Podcast Help Johnnie-O?

A podcast appearance is not a business result, and a flat month in our global rankings, where Johnnie-O currently sits 92nd, confirms that channel work rarely shows up as a spike. It shows up two seasons later as a shop that carries five styles instead of two. The AGM podcast itself is worth noting as a mechanism: a vendor putting its green grass lead in front of an audience composed almost entirely of the buyers she needs, framed as education rather than a sales call. That is a sharper piece of channel marketing than another Instagram capsule.

Watch what Johnnie-O does with its pre-book calendar and its club custom program over the next two seasons. The brands that win green grass in this cycle will be the ones that treat it as a service business rather than a wholesale account, and the tell is who they hire. Johnnie-O just hired the buyer.

What Else Do Golfers Ask About Johnnie-O?

Who is Katie Wiedmar and what does she do at Johnnie-O?

She is Johnnie-O's National Sales Manager for Green Grass Sales. Before joining the brand she spent more than ten years at Winged Foot Golf Club, including the preparation of its golf shop for the 2020 U.S. Open, and was named 2023 PGA Merchandiser of the Year in the Private category.

Why is the green grass channel so valuable to golf apparel brands?

It is the last channel that holds full margin. There is no third-party marketplace leakage, no MAP undercutting and no algorithmic discounting. The tradeoff is rationed access: a private club shop carries roughly eight to twelve apparel vendors and sets its open-to-buy months before seeing a line sheet.

Why are apparel brands hiring club buyers as sales reps?

Because buying-side experience cannot be replicated by a general apparel sales background. A former buyer understands pre-book cancellations, championship-year cash positions and the trust cost of a late shipment. AGM counts roughly 900 buyers against 200 vendors, so the talent pool is small and the brands know exactly who is in it.

Where does Johnnie-O rank in DORMIED's global brand index?

Johnnie-O currently sits 92nd globally and was flat month over month. Channel and distribution work of this type rarely produces a monthly spike; it shows up seasons later as expanded rack space in individual shops.

Why does the AGM podcast appearance matter beyond publicity?

It puts the brand's green grass lead directly in front of an audience made up almost entirely of the buyers she sells to, framed as education rather than a pitch. It is a more targeted use of marketing effort than another social capsule launch.
