Five Puerto Rico Properties on One Résumé: Troon's Rio Mar Head Pro Hire Is a Local Play

Troon hired Aaron West as head golf professional at Wyndham Grand Rio Mar. His résumé covers five Puerto Rico properties, and that is the whole point.

By Adam R.  ·  August 12, 2026  ·  Performance  ·  3 min read

Image: The Golf Wire

What Happened

Troon has named Aaron West head golf professional at Wyndham Grand Rio Mar in Rio Grande, Puerto Rico, overseeing the Fazio-designed Ocean Course and Greg Norman's River Course. West, a PGA Life Member with 25-plus years of experience, previously worked at Dorado Beach, El Conquistador, Grand Reserve, Royal Isabela and Dorado del Mar, effectively the island's entire competitive set.

Aaron West's résumé lists five Puerto Rico golf properties. On an island with maybe a dozen courses worth flying for, that is close to the entire market.

West has been named head golf professional at Wyndham Grand Rio Mar Rainforest Beach & Golf Resort in Rio Grande, where Troon runs the golf operation across two 18-hole courses: the Ocean Course, a Tom and George Fazio design playing 6,782 yards from the tips, and the Greg Norman River Course at 6,945. A PGA Life Member and Certified Professional with more than 25 years in the job, West arrives having previously worked at Dorado Beach, El Conquistador, Grand Reserve, Royal Isabela and Dorado del Mar. He holds a PGM degree from Ferris State and a master's in sport administration from Central Michigan, which is the standard-issue pedigree for people who end up running multi-course resort operations rather than teaching lessons off a mat.

The interesting part is not the credential stack. It is the geography. Troon operates at more than 950 locations in over 40 countries and has no shortage of ambitious assistants in Scottsdale who would take a Caribbean posting tomorrow. The company instead hired the person who has already worked for most of Rio Mar's competitive set. Grand Reserve hosts the PGA Tour's Puerto Rico Open. Dorado Beach is a Ritz-Carlton Reserve. Royal Isabela is the boutique cliffside play that gets the magazine spreads. West has been inside all three. That is not a hospitality hire, it is a competitive intelligence hire, and management companies make it more often than their press releases ever admit.

There is a practical reason island resort golf rewards local operators. Staffing is the hardest line item on the P&L, and turnover in a market that small runs on relationships rather than job boards. Add bilingual instruction, a tournament calendar that leans heavily on local and corporate events rather than destination outings, and the recurring reality of hurricane-season course recovery, and the value of someone who already knows which agronomy crew answers the phone in October becomes obvious. A career operator parachuting in from the mainland spends eighteen months learning what West already knows.

The broader signal is about where resort golf competes now. Puerto Rico sells itself as a domestic-flight, no-passport Caribbean golf trip, and Rio Mar's pitch is scale: 607 acres, ten restaurants, a casino, five pools, a rainforest next door. Scale is easy to advertise and hard to differentiate, because every large resort in the region has the same brochure. What actually separates them is whether the golf operation feels run rather than merely open. Tee sheet pace, staff who recognize a repeat guest, a range that is not out of balls at 8:30 a.m., a caddie program that works. Those are head professional outputs, and the resorts that lose the golf-trip dollar usually lose it there rather than on architecture.

For Troon, this is also a reminder of how the management model earns its fee. The company's growth story is usually told through door count and food and beverage revenue, but the retention story is told through hires like this one, where the operator delivers a person the ownership group could not have recruited on its own. Wyndham does not have a golf recruiting department. Troon does, and it has one that knows the Caribbean labor pool by name.

Watch what happens to Rio Mar's tournament and group calendar over the next two seasons. If West brings the corporate and association business that follows a well-connected pro around a small market, the hire pays for itself several times over and Troon will point at it the next time an owner asks what the management fee actually buys. If the calendar looks the same in 2028, this was a résumé, not a strategy.

Frequently Asked Questions

Who is Aaron West?

A PGA Life Member and Certified Professional with more than 25 years of experience, now head golf professional at Wyndham Grand Rio Mar. He has previously worked at Dorado Beach, El Conquistador, Grand Reserve, Royal Isabela and Dorado del Mar.

What courses does Rio Mar have?

Two. The Ocean Course, designed by Tom and George Fazio, plays 6,782 yards from the back tees, and the River Course, designed by Greg Norman, plays 6,945 yards.

Why does hiring locally matter for a Caribbean resort golf operation?

Staffing in a small island market runs on relationships rather than job boards, and the job also involves bilingual instruction, a locally driven tournament calendar and hurricane-season course recovery. Someone already embedded in the market skips roughly eighteen months of learning curve.

What does Troon get out of a hire like this?

Retention. Wyndham does not have a golf recruiting department and Troon does, so delivering a candidate the ownership group could not have sourced itself is a direct justification of the management fee.

How will we know if the hire worked?

Watch Rio Mar's tournament and group calendar over the next two seasons. A well-connected professional in a small market should bring corporate and association business with him.
