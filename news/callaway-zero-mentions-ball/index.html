Zero Mentions for the Ball Callaway Built for This Exact Golfer

Callaway's Chrome Soft was built for the aging premium ball player. In a MyGolfSpy case study of exactly that golfer, nobody mentioned it. Only Supersoft came up.

By Travis R.  ·  August 17, 2026  ·  Clubs  ·  3 min read

Image: MyGolfSpy

What Happened

A MyGolfSpy case study on a 25-year Pro V1x player losing driver distance to arthritis produced recommendations for Bridgestone TOUR B RXS, Maxfli Tour X and Pro V1. Callaway appeared only as Supersoft, which respondents called too big a jump. Chrome Soft, launched in 2015 at roughly 65 compression for this exact golfer, went unmentioned, a positioning problem created when Chrome Tour took the tour slot in 2024.

A 25-year Pro V1x loyalist with arthritis in his hands and fading driver distance is the single most valuable customer profile in the golf ball business, and when MyGolfSpy put his case to its forum and then to Tony Covey, Callaway showed up exactly once: as the Supersoft, the ball everyone agreed was too big a jump.

The case study is straightforward. The golfer wants softer feel, will accept an extra club into greens, and refuses to give up greenside spin. He was weighing Supersoft against Wilson DUO Soft. The forum talked him out of both, pointing instead toward Pro V1, Maxfli Tour and Tour S, Bridgestone TOUR B RX and RXS, Vice Pro Air, TaylorMade Tour Response and Srixon Q-STAR Tour. Covey landed on the Bridgestone TOUR B RXS as the primary recommendation, with Maxfli Tour X as the test-it-anyway option if firmer feel doesn't bother him. Chrome Soft never came up.

That omission is the story, because Chrome Soft is the ball Callaway launched in 2015 for precisely this person: urethane cover, tour-adjacent short game, roughly 65 compression, sold on the premise that a premium ball no longer had to feel like a rock. It worked. Chrome Soft took real share from the Pro V1 franchise and forced every OEM to build a softer four-piece. A decade later, in an unprompted thread of golfers volunteering soft premium balls, it did not make the list.

Part of that is Callaway's own doing. When Chrome Tour and Chrome Tour X arrived in 2024 and absorbed the tour positioning that Chrome Soft X and X LS used to carry, Chrome Soft was left holding a slot it no longer had language for. It isn't the tour ball anymore. It isn't the value ball, because Supersoft has owned that aisle since 2014 on the strength of a compression number in the 30s and a price point that moves cases. Chrome Soft is the middle child of a three-tier lineup, and middle children don't get recommended in forum threads. Bridgestone, meanwhile, has been selling the RX line to sub-105 swing speeds since the B330-RX era and backing it with a ball-fitting program measured in the millions of fittings. That is not better engineering. It is a clearer sentence.

There's a second, less comfortable implication. Callaway won the argument that soft sells, then discovered the argument cuts against its own premium tier. Supersoft is the most successful low-compression ball in the category and it trained a generation of golfers to believe that losing speed means buying the lowest number on the shelf. Covey's whole point is that the equation is wrong: compression is one input, and spin, launch and trajectory decide whether an aging player actually gains anything off the tee. Every time that correction gets made in public, the ball being corrected against is Supersoft. That's brand equity working in reverse.

None of this dents Callaway's business, which is why it's easy to ignore. The brand sits fourth globally in brand visibility and its month-over-month movement was flat, which for a company this size is the profile of an incumbent, not a company in trouble. Chrome Tour is a legitimate tour ball with a legitimate tour count. Supersoft still outsells most of the balls named in that thread combined. The gap is narrower and more specific: the recommendation layer, the forum posts and fitter conversations and Reddit replies that determine what a 60-year-old with a 92 mph driver actually puts in play next spring.

Watch whether Chrome Soft gets a defined message in the next lineup cycle or gets quietly folded into the Chrome Tour family. Callaway has the product for this golfer and has had it since 2015. What it currently lacks is a reason for anyone else to say so out loud.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which balls were recommended to the Pro V1x player instead of Supersoft?

Tony Covey recommended the Bridgestone TOUR B RXS first, with Maxfli Tour X as a secondary test if firmer feel isn't an issue. The MyGolfSpy forum also pointed toward Pro V1, Maxfli Tour and Tour S, Bridgestone TOUR B RX, Vice Pro Air, TaylorMade Tour Response and Srixon Q-STAR Tour.

Why wasn't Callaway Chrome Soft mentioned?

Chrome Soft occupies an unclear slot in Callaway's lineup. Since Chrome Tour and Chrome Tour X absorbed the tour positioning in 2024, Chrome Soft is neither the tour ball nor the value ball, and Supersoft has owned the low-compression aisle since 2014.

Does a golfer losing swing speed need a lower-compression ball?

Not automatically. Compression is one input alongside spin, launch and trajectory, which together determine whether a slowing player actually gains distance. Reaching for the lowest number on the shelf can cost greenside control without adding yards.

How does Bridgestone own this recommendation slot?

Bridgestone has positioned the RX line at sub-105 mph swing speeds since the B330-RX era and supports it with a ball-fitting program measured in millions of fittings. The advantage is message clarity rather than superior engineering.
