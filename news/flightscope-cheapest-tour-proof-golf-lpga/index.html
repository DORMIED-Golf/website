The Cheapest Tour Proof in Golf Is on the LPGA. FlightScope Just Bought Three Bags of It.

FlightScope has three ambassadors playing the 2026 Solheim Cup. Why LPGA endorsements are the smartest cheap buy in the launch monitor arms race.

By Travis R.  ·  September 7, 2026  ·  Launch Monitors  ·  3 min read

Image: The Golf Wire

What Happened With FlightScope?

FlightScope named three ambassadors for the 20th Solheim Cup at Bernardus Golf in Cromvoirt, Netherlands, starting September 11: Allisen Corpuz and Megan Khang for the United States, and Maja Stark for Europe. All three use the X3C, a carbon-fiber radar reading over 50 parameters. FlightScope sits 69th on the DORMIED Index.

Launch monitor companies do not make their money from tour players. They make it from the amateurs who want the same box on the mat, and the teaching professionals who need a credible logo behind the fitting bay. Which is why the endorsement math in this category runs differently than it does in clubs or balls, and why FlightScope's Solheim Cup announcement is worth more than the two paragraphs it will get elsewhere.

Which FlightScope LPGA Ambassadors Play the Solheim Cup?

The company is promoting three ambassadors playing in the 20th Solheim Cup at Bernardus Golf in Cromvoirt, Netherlands, beginning September 11: Allisen Corpuz and Megan Khang for the United States, Maja Stark for Europe. Corpuz qualified automatically in seventh on the U.S. points list with 1,268 points and remains undefeated in Solheim foursomes alongside Nelly Korda. Khang came in as a captain's pick at 11th with 749 points, her fifth appearance, and drew Angela Stanford's line about being the straw that stirs the drink. Stark qualified through the Rolex rankings on the back of her 2025 U.S. Women's Open win. All three use the X3C, the carbon-fiber-framed radar unit that reads more than 50 parameters across full swing, chipping and putting.

Having players on both teams is the actual point of the release, and it is a specific kind of flex. The tour presence in launch monitors has been an arms race since Trackman established itself as the default reference unit on the PGA Tour range in the late 2000s, and FlightScope has spent most of the last fifteen years as the challenger in that specific room. Buying equivalent visibility on the men's side costs a great deal more than it does on the LPGA, where a team-event weekend delivers international broadcast hours, two flags, and a set of players who are genuinely in the data rather than being handed a unit for the photo. FlightScope's founder Henri Johnson has always run the company lean. This is a lean company's version of a tour program.

How Does Fusion Tracking Radar Work in the X3C?

The technical claim underneath it is not marketing dressing. The category has been split for a decade between Doppler radar, which measures ball flight but has historically needed distance and open space, and photometric camera systems like the GCQuad, which capture the strike at impact and struggle outdoors in certain light. FlightScope's Fusion Tracking combines both, and the X3C is where that architecture lives in a portable frame. That is a real engineering answer to a real category problem, and it is more interesting than the fact that three Solheim Cup players own one.

What the release does not say is where the money is. FlightScope arguably created the consumer end of this market when it put the Mevo out at a price a mid-handicapper would consider, and the category has since filled with Garmin, Rapsodo, Bushnell, Square and Full Swing all selling to the same garage. Tour proof is the only durable differentiator in a segment where the spec sheets converge every eighteen months and the hardware gets copied faster than it gets improved. The company that traces its origins to 1989 tracking projectiles for the defense industry is now competing for shelf space against a GPS watch manufacturer. Solheim ambassadors are what defending that position looks like.

What Could FlightScope Do With the Solheim Window?

The brand sits 69th globally on the DORMIED Index with a flat month-over-month reading, which is roughly what you would expect from a company that builds excellent hardware and speaks quietly about it. The Solheim window is a rare moment when a launch monitor gets to be a story rather than a product page. Watch whether FlightScope converts it into anything beyond a press release: a data feature during the broadcast, a coaching partnership, a consumer unit priced against the R50. Owning a technology and owning a category are separate problems, and only one of them is solved with a radar.

What Else Do Golfers Ask About FlightScope?

Which FlightScope unit do the Solheim Cup ambassadors use?

The X3C, a carbon-fiber-framed precision tracking radar that reads more than 50 data parameters across full swing, chipping and putting.

What is FlightScope's Fusion Tracking?

It combines Doppler radar with camera-based capture. Radar measures ball flight but historically needs space, while photometric systems capture the strike at impact and can struggle outdoors in certain light. Fusion is FlightScope's answer to both limitations.

How did Corpuz, Khang and Stark qualify for the 2026 Solheim Cup?

Corpuz qualified automatically in seventh on the U.S. points list with 1,268 points. Khang was a captain's pick at 11th with 749 points, her fifth appearance. Stark qualified automatically for Europe through the Rolex Women's World Golf Rankings.

Why does an LPGA endorsement matter for a launch monitor brand?

Tour visibility is the most durable differentiator in a category where competing spec sheets converge every eighteen months. LPGA ambassadorships deliver international broadcast presence at a fraction of the cost of equivalent PGA Tour visibility.

Who competes with FlightScope in consumer launch monitors?

Garmin, Rapsodo, Bushnell, Square and Full Swing all sell into the same consumer segment FlightScope helped create with the Mevo, alongside Trackman and Foresight at the professional end.
