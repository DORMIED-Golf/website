Three Models, One Price: Srixon Just Priced the Combo Set Instead of the Iron

Srixon adds the ZXi5+ players distance iron and prices ZXi7, ZXi5 and ZXi5+ identically at $1,499.99. Why the flat pricing targets combo-set fittings.

By Travis R.  ·  September 8, 2026  ·  Balls  ·  3 min read

Image: The Golf Wire

What Happened

Srixon's second-generation ZXi irons launch October 16 with pre-sale September 8, adding a fourth model: the ZXi5+ players distance iron. ZXi7, ZXi5 and ZXi5+ all cost $1,499.99 in seven-piece steel, flat pricing designed to make combo-set fittings simpler. ZXiR sets are $1,099.99. All heads are forged by Endo Manufacturing in Japan.

The players distance category has been the largest premium iron segment in golf since TaylorMade launched the P790 in 2017. Srixon sat it out for nine years, selling a blade, a cavity, a utility and a game-improvement set, and watching Titleist's T150, Callaway's Apex Pro and Mizuno's 245 split a market it had no entry in.

That ends on October 16, when the second-generation ZXi line arrives with a fourth model. The ZXi5+ is a fully forged hollow-body players distance iron with stronger lofts, longer blade lengths and wider soles, sitting between the ZXi5 and the game-improvement ZXiR. Pre-sale opens September 8 with complimentary personalization through launch. The rest of the release covers i-FORGED, a condensed forging and controlled heat-softening process that hardens the material around the grooves while leaving the body soft, and MainFrame iQ, an AI-assisted face structure that removes up to eight percent of face mass in the ZXi5, ZXi5+ and ZXiU.

The Category Srixon Skipped for Nine Years

Srixon's iron business has always converted better than its marketing spend suggests. The Z785 and ZX7 lines built a following among better players who had never seen a Srixon television ad, and every head in the ZXi family is forged by Endo Manufacturing in Niigata, the same facility that supplies forged heads to several OEMs who do not advertise that fact on the box. The brand has credibility with the low-handicap buyer. What it did not have was a product for the four-to-fifteen handicap who wants a forged look and a 30-degree seven iron.

Casey Shultz frames the ZXi5+ as an expansion made possible by ZXiR covering the game-improvement end, which is the polite version. The direct version is that the ZXi5 was being asked to serve two buyers at once and was losing the distance-seeking one to P790 and T250. Ryan Fox winning the Open with the irons gives the launch a tour proof point, though the more useful data will be how many ZXi5+ heads show up in combo builds at Club Champion and independent fitting shops by spring.

One Price Across Three Premium Models

The pricing is the detail worth sitting with. ZXi7, ZXi5 and ZXi5+ are all $1,499.99 for a seven-piece steel set. Graphite is $1,599.99 across the two models that offer it. ZXiR and ZXiR HL come in at $1,099.99. Three distinct construction methods, three different manufacturing costs, one identical shelf price.

That is a deliberate choice, and it is aimed at the fitting bay rather than the retail wall. When models within a family carry different per-head prices, a combo set becomes a math problem the fitter has to explain: mixing four ZXi5+ long irons into a ZXi7 short-iron set changes the invoice. Flatten the price and the conversation becomes purely about performance and gapping. Srixon has also unified the shaping, topline progression and weighting across all four models specifically to make blended sets look like one set. Mizuno has run a version of this playbook for years with its Pro line. Srixon is running it with tighter price discipline.

What the Forging Claim Actually Covers

The i-FORGED description is more specific than most metallurgy marketing, which is worth acknowledging. Strengthening the material immediately around the grooves addresses a real failure mode in thin-faced forged irons: groove wear and edge deformation on high-spin players who practice. Whether the difference is perceptible inside a normal ownership cycle is a different question, and nothing in the release quantifies it beyond "compared to the previous generation."

August is a dead month for a brand launching in October, and Srixon's visibility softened accordingly heading into the announcement window. The ZXi5+ should fix that, but the more interesting test is structural. Srixon has spent a decade being the iron brand that better players find on their own. Adding a players distance model and pricing the whole family flat is an attempt to be found by a wider buyer without losing the one it already has. Watch the combo-set attachment rate. That number will say whether this worked long before the WITB counts do.
