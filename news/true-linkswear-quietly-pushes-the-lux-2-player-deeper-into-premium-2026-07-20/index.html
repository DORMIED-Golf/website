TRUE linkswear Quietly Pushes the Lux 2 Player Deeper Into Premium Territory

TRUE linkswear adds Core White and Core Grey to its $235 Lux 2 Player, signaling a serious push into premium spiked footwear against FootJoy and G/FORE.

By Adam R.  ·  July 20, 2026  ·  Shoes  ·  3 min read

Image: The Golf Wire

Two new colorways of a $235 spiked shoe from a brand best known for barefoot-adjacent comfort is not the headline TRUE linkswear was writing five years ago. It is the headline now.

TRUE has added Core White and Core Grey to its Lux 2 Player lineup, bringing the collection to six colorways. The shoe carries TRUE's FlexBolt spiked outsole, a Wanderlux2 superfoam midsole, a laminated TRUEdura upper with a two-year waterproof warranty, and the naturally wide fit that has been the brand's structural identity since it launched out of Gig Harbor in 2009. Sizes run 7 to 15, with half sizes through 12.5.

The interesting move here is not the colorways. Colorway drops are the cheapest inventory play in footwear, a way to keep a SKU on retailer shelves without the R&D cost of a new platform. The interesting move is that TRUE is running this play on a $235 spiked shoe at all. For most of its history, TRUE was the anti-spike company, the one that let you feel the ground and walk 36 holes without a blister. Founder Rob Rigg's original pitch was closer to Vibram FiveFingers than to FootJoy. The Lux 2 Player is a different animal: Tour-inspired, laminated, waterproofed, spiked, and priced within ten dollars of what FootJoy asks for a Premiere Series Packard.

That repositioning tracks with where the premium golf shoe market has gone. G/FORE turned patent leather sneakers into a $200 category. Cuater made suede-look spikeless a country-club staple. FootJoy responded by pushing the Premiere line hard into leather and lifestyle language. TRUE, which spent a decade telling golfers to enjoy the walk, now has to compete for the golfer who also wants to enjoy the parking lot. Six colorways on a single premium SKU is how you signal to green-grass buyers that you are serious about that shelf space.

Whether the market is buying it is a different question. TRUE sits at #63 in DORMIED's global brand ranking, flat month-over-month, in a footwear category where G/FORE, FootJoy, and Nike suck most of the oxygen out of the conversation. The brand has a loyal core, particularly in the Pacific Northwest and among walking-only purists, but the Lux 2 Player is aimed at a customer who was probably never in that core to begin with. TRUE is asking a golfer cross-shopping FootJoy Premiere and Cuater The Ringer to pick the brand he associates with wide toe boxes and cart-path comfort. That is a real ask.

The product itself is not the problem. The laminated TRUEdura upper is a legitimate materials story, more supple than the synthetic leathers most competitors are using at this price, and the two-year waterproof warranty is longer than what FootJoy offers on comparable models. The Breezeway heel lock is a small but genuine construction detail that addresses the one complaint TRUE's wide-fit critics have always raised, which is heel slippage. If you actually put the shoe on, it holds up. The question is whether the golfer shopping in this tier walks into a pro shop looking for it, or walks in looking for the swoosh, the crossed clubs, or the G.

Six colorways deep on a single platform tells you TRUE is committed to the Lux 2 Player as a long-term flagship, not a one-season experiment. The next test is whether the brand can get the shoe onto a Tour player's feet on a Sunday broadcast, or into the rotation of the caddie-influencer crowd that quietly moves premium footwear more than any ad buy. Without that second-screen visibility, six colorways is a very well-made shoe waiting for a customer who already bought something else.
