Full Swing's Disappearing Act: From Tiger's Garage to Marriott's Marketing Copy

Full Swing goes unmentioned in Golfzon's Marriott resort activation, raising questions about the brand's future identity post-acquisition.

By Travis  ·  April 13, 2026  ·  Equipment  ·  1 min read

Image: The Golf Wire

A resort golf package in Palm Desert is doing more brand work for Golfzon Leadbetter than Full Swing is doing for itself. That should concern anyone who remembers when Full Swing was the simulator brand, the one with Tiger Woods in the garage and tour players lining up for launch monitor validation.

Marriott's Shadow Ridge announcement name-checks Golfzon Leadbetter Academy for its 3-Day Mini School program, complete with video analysis and instruction. Full Swing, which Golfzon acquired in 2022 to create an integrated hardware and software ecosystem, gets no mention at all. The brand that once commanded attention through tour credibility and celebrity endorsement is now invisible in its parent company's own hospitality activations. Meanwhile, the DORMIED Index has Full Swing ranked 94th globally with a score of 1 out of 100, down 19 percent month over month. The metrics confirm what the messaging already suggests: this brand is being absorbed rather than elevated.

The question is whether Full Swing becomes a legacy nameplate on Golfzon hardware or reclaims its position as the premium simulator standard. Right now, the trajectory points toward the former.

The DORMIED Take

Full Swing built its entire brand on Tiger Woods's garage. The footage, the sponsorship, the implication. Pivoting the same hardware into Marriott Vacation Clubs is a real change in audience, not just a channel addition. The hotel-and-resort customer is fundamentally different from the home-simulator buyer. Different price tolerance, different usage pattern, different reason for being on the property at all.

Full Swing is betting it can serve both without diluting either, which is a hard ask. Watch whether the consumer-facing brand voice changes in the next six months. If it gets blander, the resort deals are taking over. If it stays sharp, Full Swing has figured out how to run two playbooks.
