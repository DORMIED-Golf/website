Jackson Koivun's Winning Bag Just Made the Case for Graphite in Long Irons

Jackson Koivun's 3M Open win featured a Graphite Design Tour AD DI HY 95 in his 3-iron. The graphite-in-long-irons trend just got its proof point.

By Travis R.  ·  July 31, 2026  ·  Shafts  ·  3 min read

Image: MyGolfSpy

Jackson Koivun won the 3M Open in his third start as a professional, and buried in the bag notes is a shaft decision that matters more than the trophy. His 3-iron is a Titleist T250 with a Graphite Design Tour AD DI HY 95 X. Not the T250·U utility. The standard T250 head, built as a long iron, sleeved into a hybrid-weight graphite shaft.

Combo sets are not the story. Roughly 70 percent of PGA Tour winners this season have gamed multiple iron models. What Koivun did is a step further: three different Titleist heads across seven irons, and a material change at the top of the set. T250 with graphite at the 3, T100 with Project X 6.5 at the 4, 620 MB Prototype with Project X 6.5 from the 5-iron down. The bridge is not clean. It is engineered.

The shaft choice is where the industry should pay attention. Graphite in long irons has been a slow-motion trend on tour for four years, ever since Brendan Steele put Recoil in the top of his set and finished a season inside the top 30 in strokes gained approach. UST Mamiya, Mitsubishi and Graphite Design have all built hybrid-weight iron shafts specifically for this use case. Adoption at the tour level was quiet through 2023. It is no longer quiet. Koivun winning a tournament with a Tour AD DI HY 95 in a 3-iron gives the pitch a proof point that a fitter can hand a mid-handicap player without hedging.

For Graphite Design specifically, the placement matters. The brand has been a fixture in tour wood shafts for two decades, with the Tour AD DI still one of the most-used aftermarket wood profiles in the game. Iron and hybrid-weight graphite has been a harder sell against steel orthodoxy and against KBS and Project X in the graphite-iron space. A rookie winner running the Tour AD DI HY at 95 grams in a bladed-adjacent setup is the kind of visibility that catalog copy cannot buy. Whether it converts into fitting-bay demand is the open question. The brand's global visibility trend has been soft over the last month, down meaningfully in the DORMIED Index, which makes a tour moment like this more valuable, not less.

The amateur read is the one MyGolfSpy flagged and it is correct. Most mid-handicap players who struggle with a 3- or 4-iron are not going to be rescued by a stronger-lofted utility. They are going to be helped by a lighter shaft that lets them get the ball in the air with a repeatable delivery. The industry has known this since the first Adams Idea hybrid in 2003. What is new is that the same logic now applies inside the iron set, not just at the transition to hybrids. Koivun's bag is a walking argument for that conversation at the fitting cart.

The next twelve months will test whether Graphite Design can convert tour placement into aftermarket iron-shaft sales in a category still dominated by steel. The Tour AD DI HY has the tour proof now. What it needs is a distribution and fitting story that matches. If the brand leans into the moment with fitter education and cart placement at Club Champion and True Spec, the trajectory bends. If it treats this as another win notation on the website, the ranking keeps drifting.
