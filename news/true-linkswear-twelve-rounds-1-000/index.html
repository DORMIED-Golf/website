Twelve Rounds for $250 and 1,000 Members: TRUE linkswear Buys Into Future Golf USA

TRUE linkswear becomes Future Golf's US footwear partner as the Australian membership platform launches in Northern California at $250 a year.

By Adam R.  ·  September 23, 2026  ·  Shoes  ·  4 min read

Image: The Golf Wire

What Happened With TRUE linkswear?

TRUE linkswear is now the official U.S. footwear partner of Future Golf, the Australian membership platform launching in Northern California. Future Golf's first 1,000 Founding Memberships cost $250 a year for twelve rounds, locked for life, rising to $400 after. A launch event runs at Golden Gate Park Golf Course on October 30, 2026, with a TRUE lounge on site.

The number that matters in Future Golf's American launch is not 60,000. It is 1,000.

TRUE linkswear has been named the official U.S. footwear partner of Future Golf, the Australian multi-course membership platform that launched stateside in Northern California. Future Golf, founded in 2014, has built a network of more than 60,000 golfers in Australia around an app that handles booking, scoring, handicap tracking and events in a single place. The U.S. rollout opens with a capped run of Founding Memberships at $250 per year, twelve included rounds, price locked for life for the first 1,000 sign-ups. After that, membership goes to $400 with the same twelve rounds. The two brands will mark the launch with an event at Golden Gate Park Golf Course on October 30, 2026, where TRUE will run a branded lounge.

Why Does TRUE linkswear Need Future Golf's Membership List?

Golf footwear is the hardest category in the industry to break into from the outside, because the shelf is already spoken for. FootJoy, Nike, adidas and Skechers occupy the green-grass pro shop and the Dick's wall, and the fight for that space is decided by rep networks and volume commitments rather than by whether the shoe is good. TRUE has never played that game particularly well. It has played the direct game instead: a Pacific Northwest brand running its own pro shop and content studio out of Oakbrook Golf Club in Lakewood, selling seven core silhouettes to people who already know the name.

Which makes this less a sponsorship than a distribution deal dressed as one. Future Golf is not a tournament or a tour player. It is a list, with an app attached, of golfers who have paid up front to walk public courses more often. That is the exact customer for a shoe whose entire pitch is comfort over eighteen holes and the parking lot afterward. A logo on a Northern California membership platform does nothing for brand awareness in the broad sense. It puts the product in front of a few thousand people whose behavior has already been verified by a credit card.

What Does a $250 Founding Membership Actually Buy?

The economics are worth sitting with. Twelve rounds for $250 works out to roughly $21 a round, which in Northern California is below what most municipal courses charge for a weekday twilight. Future Golf is buying customer acquisition with course inventory, the same way ClubCorp and Troon-adjacent programs have used bundled access, except the wall here is a phone rather than an initiation fee. The lifetime price lock on the first cohort is the clever part: it manufactures urgency for a product that otherwise has no scarcity, and it gives the company a founding membership base that will evangelize because they are grandfathered.

Jason Moore's quote about dress codes written in the 1950s is on-message for a brand that has spent fifteen years positioning against the country club, and it is not wrong. But the interesting thing about this partnership is not the shared ethos language in the release. It is that both companies have the same structural problem: no retail presence, no institutional distribution, and a product that only makes sense once someone has already tried it. Pairing two brands with the same weakness does not fix it. It does make the funnel cheaper.

Can a Northern California Launch Move TRUE linkswear's Visibility?

Not quickly. TRUE sits 69th of 215 brands in the DORMIED Index with a score under two, and visibility slid more than 18 percent month over month heading into this announcement. A single-market activation with a capped membership and one nine-hole muni event is not the kind of move that reverses that. It is not designed to. This is a 2027 play that happens to have a press release in 2026.

What to watch is whether Future Golf expands beyond the Bay Area and whether TRUE's deal travels with it. The Australian partnership has a decade of operating history behind it, which is more proof of concept than most golf tech platforms bring to a U.S. launch. If Future Golf lands in Phoenix, Austin and the Carolinas over the next two years with TRUE attached, the brand will have built a national distribution channel that never required a single pro shop order. If it stalls at 1,000 members in one metro, TRUE will have paid for a lounge.

What Else Do Golfers Ask About TRUE linkswear?

What does a Future Golf Founding Membership cost?

$250 per year, including twelve rounds across the Future Golf network. The first 1,000 Founding Members keep that price for life. Standard memberships afterward are $400 per year with the same twelve rounds.

Where is Future Golf launching in the United States?

Northern California, with an inaugural event at Golden Gate Park Golf Course on October 30, 2026. TRUE linkswear will run a branded lounge at the event.

Why does the partnership make sense for TRUE linkswear?

TRUE has minimal green-grass and big-box retail presence and sells largely direct. Future Golf gives it access to a verified list of golfers who have already paid to walk public courses, which is the brand's core customer.

How established is Future Golf outside the US?

It was founded in Australia in 2014 and has grown to more than 60,000 golfers, making it the country's largest golf community. TRUE linkswear was already a partner there before the US expansion.
