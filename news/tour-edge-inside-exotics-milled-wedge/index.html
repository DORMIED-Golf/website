Beaten in Its Own Spin Test and Published It Anyway: Inside Tour Edge's $110 Exotics Milled Wedge

Tour Edge launches Exotics Milled wedges Sept. 14 at $110 with three bounce options, milled grooves, and internal testing the brand admits it lost.

By Travis R.  ·  September 1, 2026  ·  Clubs  ·  3 min read

Image: MyGolfSpy

What Happened With Tour Edge?

Tour Edge launches its Exotics Milled wedge line Sept. 14 at $110, with three bounce profiles across 50 to 60 degrees, cast 304 stainless heads, CNC-milled SuperSpin grooves and a three-part TriGrind sole. Stock build includes a KBS Hi-Rev 2.0 115-gram shaft. Internal testing showed competitors out-spinning it in dry conditions, published anyway.

The barrier to entry in wedges has never been casting. It is SKU count. Three bounce options across lofts from 50 to 60 degrees produces north of 20 stock configurations before anyone builds a left-handed run, and every one of those heads sits in a warehouse until a fitter puts it in a customer's hands. That inventory math is why value brands historically stop at one grind and call it a wedge line.

What Are the Exotics Milled Bounce Options and Price?

Tour Edge is no longer stopping. The Exotics Milled wedge line hits retail Sept. 14 at $110 per club, with three bounce profiles: LB in 54, 58 and 60 degrees, MB across the full 50 to 60 range, and HB from 54 through 60. The heads are cast 304 stainless with CNC-milled faces, grooves and sole details, packing more grooves onto the face at maximum conforming depth under the name SuperSpin. The sole is a three-part shape Tour Edge calls TriGrind: chamfered leading edge, flatter mid-sole, heavy trailing-edge relief.

The context here is 40 years of a company known for one thing. David Glod built Tour Edge on fairway woods and hybrids, and the Exotics line spent two decades as the answer to a specific question about the 3-wood slot. The wedge box stayed unchecked. The Wingman forged wedge co-designed with Bernhard Langer offers three grinds but starts at 52 degrees in a high-toe shape and sits well above this price point. What was missing was a mainstream, fitting-friendly wedge with a bounce conversation attached. That is the actual product being launched here, not a groove.

Why Did Tour Edge Publish Losing Spin Data?

The more interesting disclosure is in the testing document. Tour Edge published internal spin data showing both unnamed competitors out-spinning the 56-degree Exotics Milled from 40 yards, by 60 and 155 rpm, and one competitor beating it by nearly 300 rpm from 70 yards. OEMs do not publish losses. The last comparable instance was Cleveland reporting a Turbo Launcher fairway wood as the second-longest carry in its own test, then explaining the total-distance case. The wet-condition numbers, where the Exotics gave up under 950 rpm at 70 yards against 1,600 to 4,000 for the competition, are worth substantially more because of the dry numbers printed above them. Credibility is the only currency a smaller OEM can spend against Vokey.

Which brings up the pricing problem, and it is a problem. At $110 with a KBS Hi-Rev 2.0 115-gram shaft and Golf Pride Tour Velvet 360 stock, Tour Edge is roughly 40 percent under where premium wedges from the majors now sit, with no downgrade in the build. Consumers read cheap as worse, particularly in a category where the purchase is emotional and the incumbent has spent 25 years teaching golfers that grind selection is an act of self-knowledge. Marketing VP Jon Claffey has been explicit that the brief is to stop leading with price and let it land at checkout. That only works if the club gets into a fitting cart first, which is exactly where Tour Edge's green-grass and independent-shop distribution is stronger than its shelf presence at big-box.

Why Could Wedges Change Tour Edge's Visibility?

Tour Edge sits at #48 globally and moved zero percent month over month, which is the signature of a brand producing good product without generating conversation. Wedges are the category most likely to change that, because they are bought more often than drivers, discussed more obsessively, and evaluated on feel that a fitting bay can actually deliver. The thing to watch after Sept. 14 is not review scores. It is whether Tour Edge holds $110 through the first discount cycle. A value brand that starts promoting a $110 wedge in February has told the market exactly what it thinks the club is worth.

What Else Do Golfers Ask About Tour Edge?

What do the Tour Edge Exotics Milled wedges cost and when do they launch?

They hit retail and online Sept. 14 at $110 per club. That is roughly 40 percent below where premium wedges from the major OEMs currently sit, with a KBS Hi-Rev 2.0 115-gram shaft and Golf Pride Tour Velvet 360 grip as the stock build.

What bounce and loft options are available?

Three profiles. LB, the low-bounce option with extra heel relief, comes in 54, 58 and 60 degrees. MB, the mid-bounce all-arounder, spans the full 50 to 60 range. HB, the high-bounce option, is offered from 54 through 60.

Did Tour Edge really publish testing its own wedge lost?

Yes. In dry 40-yard testing with a 56-degree Exotics Milled, both unnamed competitors spun more, by 60 and 155 rpm. At 70 yards one competitor beat it by nearly 300 rpm. The wedge won decisively in wet conditions, losing under 950 rpm at 70 yards against 1,600 to 4,000 for the competition.

How are the Exotics Milled wedges built?

The heads are cast from 304 stainless steel, with faces, grooves and sole details CNC-milled afterward. Tour Edge says that process delivers a tighter groove edge radius and steeper sidewalls, and the SuperSpin grooves sit closer together at maximum conforming depth.

Why is this launch significant for Tour Edge specifically?

The brand built its identity over four decades on fairway woods and hybrids, and wedges were the last unchecked box in a full bag. The Langer-designed forged Wingman offers grinds but starts at 52 degrees in a high-toe shape at a higher price. Exotics Milled is the first mainstream, fitting-friendly wedge line with a real bounce conversation attached.
