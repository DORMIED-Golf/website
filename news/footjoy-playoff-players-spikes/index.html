Nine of Ten Playoff Players Wear Spikes. Retail Went the Other Way, and FootJoy Sells Both.

Softspikes hit 31 straight PGA TOUR wins with FootJoy footwear, and 62 of 70 Playoff qualifiers wear spikes. The retail floor went spikeless. Here is the split.

By James K.  ·  August 10, 2026  ·  Shoes  ·  3 min read

Image: The Golf Wire

What Happened

Softspikes extended its PGA TOUR winning streak to 31 consecutive weeks at the Wyndham Championship, where the winner wore FootJoy footwear with Tour Flex Pro spikes and 43 of the top 47 finishers used replaceable traction. Of 70 FedExCup Playoff qualifiers, 62 wear spikes. The real business is the 15-round replacement cycle that spikeless outsoles eliminate entirely.

Every golf product wants a replacement cycle. Softspikes has one, and it is the reason a spike company issues a press release every single Monday of the PGA TOUR season.

The latest one puts the streak at 31 consecutive weeks. The Wyndham Championship winner took a three-shot victory in FootJoy footwear fitted with Tour Flex Pro spikes, extending a run in which a player wearing replaceable traction has won every week of the 2026 season. The field numbers are the more interesting part: 43 of the top 47 finishers at Sedgefield wore replaceable spikes, and of the 70 players who qualified for the FedExCup Playoffs, just eight are in spikeless or tennis-style shoes. Softspikes also collected a Korn Ferry Tour win at the Pinnacle Bank Championship, where the winner shot a final-round 59 in FootJoy fitted with Pulsars and won in a playoff.

Worth understanding what these counts actually measure. Softspikes is manufactured by PrideSports, and its business is not shoes, it is the small plastic part inside somebody else's shoes. That makes the tour count a proxy for two things at once: how many elite players still want a cleat, and how much of tour footwear share belongs to FootJoy. Those two numbers travel together, because FootJoy's spiked lineup is where the majority of the count lives. Joe Henderson, Softspikes president, called 31 weeks a demonstration of consistency at the highest level. It is also a demonstration that the aftermarket's health depends on a shoe category the retail floor has been quietly walking away from.

The most revealing number in the release is not 31 or 62. It is 15. Softspikes recommends replacing spikes every 15 rounds, and that cadence is the entire business model: a razor-and-blade structure attached to a $180 to $250 shoe. A spikeless outsole ends that relationship permanently. Nobody has ever bought a replacement set of rubber nubs for a Pro SL. Every spikeless pair sold is not just a lost cleat sale, it is a lost decade of cleat sales, which is why the traction category defends its tour share this aggressively and this publicly.

FootJoy occupies both sides of the trade, and does so deliberately. Premiere Series and the tour-spec models carry replaceable traction for the players who need it on wet Bermuda in August. Pro SL and HyperFlex sit on the wall for the golfer who parks 40 yards from the first tee, rides 18 holes, and stops at the grill. That golfer is the volume, and the volume went spikeless years ago on comfort and convenience, not performance. The tour-versus-retail divergence here is wider than in almost any other equipment category. Nobody at Sedgefield is playing a driver the buying public rejected.

The honest performance argument, and the one Softspikes makes almost in passing, is the more useful one for anyone reading this: amateurs do not replace spikes. Ever. A set of cleats ground flat over four seasons delivers less traction than a well-designed spikeless outsole, which means the tour-count argument only holds for golfers who maintain the hardware. That is a maintenance pitch dressed as a performance pitch, and it is the version a fitter or a pro shop can actually sell without overpromising.

For FootJoy, sitting fifth of 215 brands tracked in the DORMIED Index but down 18.5 percent month over month, tour counts are a lagging indicator of a conversation happening somewhere else. The brand's footwear authority has never been in question at the tour level. The question is what happens to the spiked side of the range when the players who justify it are 70 guys and the customers who buy it are a shrinking share of a floor dominated by athletic-looking spikeless silhouettes. Watch the SKU count. If FootJoy's spiked models narrow to a tour-and-tournament-player tier over the next two seasons, the streak will keep running and the aftermarket underneath it will keep getting smaller anyway.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long is the Softspikes PGA TOUR winning streak?

31 consecutive weeks, covering every event of the 2026 season to date. The most recent was the Wyndham Championship, won by three shots in FootJoy footwear fitted with Tour Flex Pro spikes.

How many FedExCup Playoff qualifiers wear spikeless shoes?

Eight of the 70 qualifiers. The other 62 are competing in replaceable spikes, which is roughly nine out of ten players entering the postseason.

Why does a spike company publish tour win counts every week?

Softspikes is made by PrideSports and sells a consumable part rather than a shoe. Its revenue depends on golfers buying spiked footwear and then replacing the cleats, so tour share is the credibility argument for a category retail has been moving away from.

How often should golfers replace their spikes?

Softspikes recommends every 15 rounds. That cadence is the business model, and it is also the catch in the performance argument: worn-flat cleats can offer less traction than a well-designed spikeless outsole.

Where does FootJoy sit in this split?

On both sides of it. Premiere Series and tour-spec models carry replaceable traction, while Pro SL and HyperFlex serve the spikeless retail volume. FootJoy currently ranks fifth of 215 brands tracked, down 18.5 percent month over month.
