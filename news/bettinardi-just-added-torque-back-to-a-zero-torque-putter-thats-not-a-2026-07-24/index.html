Bettinardi Just Added Torque Back to a Zero-Torque Putter. That's Not a Small Signal.

Bettinardi's new Antidote Hexperimental #2 adds torque back to a zero-torque putter, the first meaningful counter-move in a category chasing L.A.B. Golf.

By Travis R.  ·  July 24, 2026  ·  Putters  ·  3 min read

Image: MyGolfSpy

What Happened With Bettinardi?

Bettinardi's new Antidote Hexperimental #2 takes the SB2, a center-shafted zero-torque mallet, and swaps in a plumber's neck hosel that reintroduces toe hang. It is the first meaningful counter-move since L.A.B. Golf opened the zero-torque category in 2022, when Odyssey, TaylorMade, PXG, Scotty Cameron, Evnroll, and Bettinardi itself all engineered torque out of mallets.

Every major putter OEM spent the last three years engineering torque out of their mallets. Bettinardi just engineered it back into one.

What Changes on the Bettinardi Antidote Hexperimental #2?

The new Antidote Hexperimental #2 is, at first glance, a minor line extension. Bettinardi took the SB2, a center-shafted zero-torque mallet from the original Hexperimental drop, and swapped the hosel for a plumber's neck. The three-part 303 stainless and 6061 aluminum body carries over. The F.I.T. face milling carries over. The two-tone topline carries over. What changes is the torque profile: the plumber's neck reintroduces toe hang and, with it, the exact characteristic the original SB2 was designed to eliminate.

The direction of that swap is the story. Since L.A.B. Golf forced the category open in 2022, essentially every major putter brand has moved the same way. Odyssey Square 2 Square. TaylorMade Spider ZT. PXG Allan. Scotty's Phantom 5.5 and 11.5 with the counterbalance shafts. Evnroll Zero. Bettinardi's own original Hexperimental line in April. The design brief across every one of those releases was the same: take a traditional head, kill the torque, chase the L.A.B. buyer. Bettinardi just released the first meaningful counter-move, taking a zero-torque head and adding torque back.

Why Did Bettinardi Add Torque Back to the SB2?

The commercial logic is straightforward. Zero-torque putters test well in aggregate, MyGolfSpy's 2026 data confirms it, but aggregate performance is not fitting performance. Plenty of players who have released the putter the same way for twenty years do not suddenly putt better when the face stays open through impact. Bettinardi is essentially acknowledging that its own SB2 shape has fans who bounced off the center-shaft build, and giving those buyers a version that fits their stroke. That's fitting-bay math, not a philosophical statement.

But it is worth asking whether the zero-torque category is closer to saturation than the release cadence suggests. Used-bin turnover is the honest metric here, and there are a lot of barely-used zero-torque putters moving through secondary channels right now. The category grew faster than any putter subsegment since the mallet boom of the mid-2000s, and history says a category that grows that fast tends to normalize. The 2005 to 2008 mallet wave produced the Odyssey 2-Ball, the White Hot XG, the original Spider. By 2012, tour counts had rebalanced and the blade came back into fashion. Trends in this category rarely die. They just stop being the only thing brands ship.

What Is Bettinardi Signaling to Zero-Torque Putter Buyers?

Bettinardi is a useful brand to watch on this specifically because it is not a volume player. At a DORMIED global rank of 36, the brand operates in a tier where every SKU has to justify itself against a small, discerning customer base that talks to each other on WRX and at fitting studios. Bettinardi does not have the shelf space to chase a fading trend. If the Hexperimental line is where the company signals what it thinks is next, and Sam Bettinardi's quote frames it that way, then adding a plumber's neck to a zero-torque head is a small but deliberate hedge.

The Phantom 11.5 and the Spider Tour plumber's neck have been quietly stable sellers through the entire zero-torque cycle. Mallets that play like blades never really left. Bettinardi is betting that customer is still there, and that the fitting conversation two years from now sounds less like "do you want zero-torque" and more like "what stroke are we actually building around." That's a more mature version of the category, and it's probably where it lands. The Hexperimental #2 is not the inflection point. It's the first release that acts like the inflection point is coming.

What exactly is different about the Antidote Hexperimental #2 versus the SB2?

The three-part 303 stainless and 6061 aluminum body, F.I.T. face milling, and two-tone topline all carry over from the SB2. The only meaningful change is the hosel: a plumber's neck replaces the center shaft, reintroducing toe hang to a head originally designed to eliminate it.

Why did Bettinardi add torque back to a zero-torque putter?

The article frames it as fitting-bay math. Zero-torque putters test well in aggregate per MyGolfSpy's 2026 data, but players with established strokes do not automatically putt better with a face that stays open through impact. Bettinardi is serving SB2 fans who bounced off the center-shaft build.

Which zero-torque releases preceded this counter-move?

The article cites the Odyssey Square 2 Square, TaylorMade Spider ZT, PXG Allan, Scotty Cameron Phantom 5.5 and 11.5 with counterbalance shafts, Evnroll Zero, and Bettinardi's own original Hexperimental line released in April.

Why does Bettinardi's move matter given the brand's size?

Bettinardi holds a DORMIED global rank of 36 and is not a volume player. Every SKU has to justify itself to a small, discerning customer base, so the brand cannot afford to chase a fading trend, which makes the plumber's neck addition a deliberate hedge.

What historical parallel does the article draw?

The article compares the zero-torque surge to the 2005 to 2008 mallet wave that produced the Odyssey 2-Ball, White Hot XG, and original Spider. By 2012, tour counts rebalanced and blades returned to fashion, suggesting fast-growing putter categories tend to normalize rather than dominate permanently.
