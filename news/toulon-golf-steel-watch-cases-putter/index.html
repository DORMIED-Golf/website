The Steel Rolex Uses for Watch Cases Is Now a Toulon Putter. There Are Two.

Toulon Golf breaks its one-at-a-time pattern with two Small Batch Midwest putters: the 904L steel Chicago and the cavity-backed pintail Milwaukee.

By Travis R.  ·  September 24, 2026  ·  Putters  ·  4 min read

Image: MyGolfSpy

What Happened With Toulon Golf?

Toulon Golf released two Small Batch Midwest putters at once, breaking its one-at-a-time pattern. The Chicago is milled from 904L stainless, the steel Rolex uses, with a double-bend shaft and about 10 degrees of toe hang. The Milwaukee is a cavity-backed pintail with roughly 60 degrees. Both are limited, historically priced around $1,800 to $2,000.

904L stainless is what Rolex machines its Oyster cases from, and it cuts at roughly half the pace of the 303 stainless most milled putters start life as. It work-hardens under the tool, chews through cutters and punishes anyone in a hurry. Toulon Golf just milled a putter head out of it.

That putter is the Small Batch Chicago, and it arrives alongside the Small Batch Milwaukee as the Midwest release. It is the first time the Small Batch series has dropped two heads at once. Previous releases, including last month's Coastal California run, came one at a time. Quantities are minimal and pricing has not been formally stated, though the series has historically landed in the $1,800 to $2,000 window.

Why Does 904L Steel Change the Math on a Milled Putter?

Material choice in milled putters is usually a feel argument: 1018 carbon for softness, 303 for durability, German stainless when somebody wants to charge more. 904L is a different conversation. It exists because of corrosion resistance in marine and chemical environments, and it sits in a Rolex case because Rolex wanted a polish that holds. Nobody selected it for roll quality. Toulon selected it because the buyer for a $2,000 putter reads watch forums, and 904L is a word that means something there.

The rest of the Chicago is more conventional and more interesting. The wide-body shape echoes the fully milled 2020 Odyssey/Toulon Chicago, a club that vanished from the lineup when Sean Toulon retired and Odyssey's milled offerings went with him. The face carries Ultra Double Fly Cut milling under a black PVD finish. The double-bend shaft is a first for the Small Batch line and produces roughly 10 degrees of toe hang. The sole is covered in 34 polka dots, a Buddy Guy Stratocaster reference that also happens to be Walter Payton's number. That is two stories stacked into one sole plate, which is what this category sells.

What Does Doubling the Small Batch Drop Say About Toulon Golf?

The Milwaukee is the more engineered of the pair. It is a pintail, a shape Toulon returns to often, but this one carries a cavity that the Portrush and Dogwood pintails did not. A cavity on a pintail is not decoration; it moves mass, changes MOI and alters the sound. The slant neck delivers about 60 degrees of toe hang. Put the two releases side by side and the actual logic surfaces: 10 degrees and 60 degrees is the full width of the arc spectrum. Toulon did not release two putters that compete with each other. It released one drop that catches both stroke types in the same window.

That is a supply decision dressed as a creative one. Limited-run putter brands live and die on drop cadence. Scotty Cameron's Custom Shop built the modern version of this business, Bettinardi and Byron Morgan run their own variations, and the mechanic is borrowed wholesale from sneakers and watches: constrain supply, let the secondary market set the real price, and never satisfy demand completely. Two heads per drop instead of one means Toulon believes its list has grown faster than its release schedule.

Who Is Actually Buying a $2,000 Toulon Golf Putter?

Not a golfer looking for a putter. A collector who already has a gamer and wants an object. Toulon Golf operates without meaningful retail shelf space, without an OEM tour budget and without a volume line to subsidize the small stuff, which is the inverse of how the brand functioned inside Callaway. Scarcity is the entire marketing department. A 22 percent month-over-month move in DORMIED's global brand rankings, from a small base at 89th of 215, is what that looks like when a drop lands.

The question for the next twelve months is whether a drop business becomes a brand business. Sean Toulon has run volume before and knows exactly what shelf presence costs and what it returns. Doubling the Midwest release is the first visible sign that the current model is capacity-constrained rather than demand-constrained. Watch for either a faster cadence or, more tellingly, a non-limited core line. One of those funds a factory. The other builds something that outlasts the founder's name on the sole.

What Else Do Golfers Ask About Toulon Golf?

What is different about the Toulon Small Batch Midwest release?

It is the first Small Batch drop to include two putters at once. Previous releases, including the Coastal California series, came one model at a time.

Why did Toulon Golf use 904L steel in the Chicago putter?

904L is best known as the alloy Rolex uses for its Oyster cases. It was not developed for putter feel, and it machines slower than the 303 stainless most milled putters use. The choice speaks to the collector buyer more than to roll performance.

How much do the Toulon Small Batch Chicago and Milwaukee cost?

Pricing was not formally stated, but the Small Batch series has historically landed in the $1,800 to $2,000 range.

How do the two putters differ in toe hang?

The Chicago, with its double-bend shaft, has about 10 degrees of toe hang and suits a minimal-arc stroke. The Milwaukee's slant neck produces roughly 60 degrees, fitting a significantly arced stroke.

What makes the Milwaukee different from earlier Toulon pintails?

It has a cavity, which the Portrush and Dogwood pintails did not. That changes mass distribution, MOI, sound and the look at address.
