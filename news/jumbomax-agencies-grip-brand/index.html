Two Agencies for a Grip Brand. JumboMax Is Buying Its Way Out of the Bryson Box.

JumboMax names JDPR its PR agency-of-record while keeping Rooted Solutions, a two-agency spend aimed at outgrowing its Bryson DeChambeau identity.

By James K.  ·  August 20, 2026  ·  Grips  ·  3 min read

Image: The Golf Wire

What Happened With JumboMax?

JumboMax Golf Grips has named JDPR its North American public relations agency-of-record, while Rooted Solutions continues to lead brand and growth marketing after more than six years. JDPR's golf roster includes Cobra Puma Golf, Bridgestone Golf and FlightScope. The two-agency spend signals a repositioning away from JumboMax's Bryson DeChambeau association and toward an ergonomic fitting argument aimed at a far larger market.

The fastest way to explain JumboMax to a golfer is to say "the Bryson grips," and that shorthand is worth a fortune right up until the moment it becomes the ceiling. Ten years of being the oversize outlier attached to the most visible equipment eccentric in the sport has bought the company enormous awareness and a very narrow identity. The company just hired an agency to do something about that.

Why Did JumboMax Hire JDPR as Its Golf PR Agency?

JumboMax has named JDPR its public relations agency-of-record for North America, covering strategic planning, media relations and product launch work. Rooted Solutions, which has run brand and growth marketing for JumboMax for more than six years, stays in place. JDPR brings a golf roster that includes Cobra Puma Golf, Bridgestone Golf and FlightScope, and a category history it dates to 35 years. Two agencies on retainer is a serious line item for a company whose unit of sale is a rubber sleeve that retails for the price of two sleeves of balls.

The structural problem behind the spend is worth spelling out. The grip category has exactly one durable moat, and it is stock installation. Golf Pride wins because its grips arrive on the club before the golfer has an opinion, which turns a preference into a default across millions of units a year. JumboMax cannot have that moat and never will, because an oversize grip is by definition a fitting decision. Nobody ships a driver with a JumboMax XL on it as standard. Every sale the company makes has to be converted one hand at a time, in a fitting bay or a shopping cart, against a grip the golfer already owns and did not choose.

How Is JumboMax Moving Beyond Bryson DeChambeau Grips?

Which makes the language in the announcement more interesting than the announcement. The pitch is "proper ergonomic fit" and selecting size, shape and weight based on fit and performance. That is a club fitting argument, not a speed argument, and it is a deliberate move away from the tour board that has carried the brand. Look at the names on that board: Bryson DeChambeau, Martin Borgmeier, Justin James, Kelly Rudney. Long drive specialists and one physicist. Jason Day and Retief Goosen are the counterweights, and Goosen is the more useful of the two precisely because nobody has ever accused him of trying to hit it 340. A brand that reads as equipment for people who swing at 200 mph ball speed has a small addressable market. A brand that reads as correctly sized grips for hands that are not the mid-century average has a very large one, and it happens to sit directly on top of where golf's participation growth actually is.

There is a distribution argument here too. Re-gripping is one of the last equipment transactions that still requires labor, which means the green grass shop and the independent fitter retain real influence over what goes on the club. That audience does not read consumer launch coverage. It reads trade, it talks to reps, and it decides what to stock based partly on whether customers walk in already knowing the name. Hiring an earned-media shop with existing OEM relationships is what a brand does when it needs buyers and fitters to have heard the story before the sales call, not after. It currently sits 85th of 215 in the DORMIED Index with no movement month over month, and flat is precisely the condition that gets an agency hired.

What Would Show JumboMax Has Changed Its Story?

The thing to watch over the next twelve months is not how much coverage JumboMax generates but what the coverage is about. If the stories are still Bryson stories, the retainer bought volume and nothing else. If the stories are about hand size, arthritis, grip pressure and why the standard grip diameter is an inherited assumption rather than a spec, then the brand has done the harder thing, which is to detach itself from the player who made it famous while he is still playing well.
