Seven Scripted Days and a Women's Line With No Track Record: B. Draddy Picked a Hard Debut

B. Draddy and Zero Restriction revealed the 2026 U.S. Solheim Cup uniforms, using a 13-year outerwear deal to launch a brand new women's line.

By Adam R.  ·  September 8, 2026  ·  Luxury/High-End  ·  3 min read

Image: The Golf Wire

What Happened

B. Draddy and Zero Restriction unveiled the 2026 U.S. Solheim Cup Team uniforms for the September 11 to 13 matches at Bernardus Golf in Cromvoirt, Netherlands. All seven days are scripted, with throwbacks to the 1996, 2007 and 2015 teams. It marks the tournament debut of Draddy Sport for Women, and Zero Restriction's seventh straight Solheim Cup as outerwear supplier since 2013.

Zero Restriction has dressed the U.S. Solheim Cup team at every playing since 2013. The apparel line making its tournament debut alongside it in the Netherlands this September is barely old enough to have a second season of inventory.

B. Draddy and Zero Restriction, both owned by Summit Golf Brands, revealed the official uniforms for the 2026 U.S. Solheim Cup Team, which competes September 11 to 13 at Bernardus Golf in Cromvoirt, with team week beginning the 7th. The two brands were named official uniform suppliers back in November 2025 in a deal covering 2026 and 2028. They scripted all seven days plus off-course looks, and three of those days are throwbacks to specific past U.S. teams: Monday to 2015, Wednesday to 2007, Thursday to 1996. The headline for B. Draddy is that this is the first competitive appearance of Draddy Sport for Women.

That is the actual business decision here. Summit had a thirteen-year incumbent relationship on outerwear and used it as a launchpad for a brand new women's performance line rather than buying visibility the way most apparel brands do, one LPGA player contract at a time. A single top-20 player deal buys you a logo on one chest for a season. Scripting a Solheim Cup team buys you twelve players, two captains, an assistant staff and seven days of broadcast in Europe, with the throwback story functioning as its own press cycle. Launching a women's line at the Solheim Cup is like opening a restaurant by catering a wedding: captive audience, zero margin for error.

What gets underweighted in reads of deals like this is that team uniform contracts are fulfillment contracts before they are design contracts. Seven scripted days, custom event marks, USA logos, a team flag, and three archival looks reconstructed from 1996, 2007 and 2015 means hundreds of decorated SKUs produced against a roster that is not final until late in qualifying, then revised again when someone's size or preference changes on site. Summit runs more than 200 embroidery heads across 75,000-plus square feet in Madison and Barneveld, Wisconsin. That vertical integration is the reason they can bid this work at all, and it is a harder thing for a competitor to replicate than a good polo pattern.

The product mix suggests someone thought about the actual week rather than the lookbook. Moisture-wicking stretch for the warm afternoons, breathable cotton for the off-course appearances, and cashmere sweaters from the B. Draddy Women's Collection for the mornings when the Dutch coast decides it is October. Zero Restriction supplies the layers that matter most in a September event in the Low Countries: quarter zips, crewnecks, rain jackets and rain pants. Rain pants are the least glamorous item in any scripting package and the one most likely to appear on camera during a Friday four-ball delay. Getting the waist articulation right on those does more for the brand's credibility with a caddie than any polo will.

The skeptical note is on the retail side. Both brands are selling the uniforms to fans at bdraddy.com and zerorestriction.com, and Solheim Cup fan merchandise has never been the volume business the Ryder Cup version is. The more valuable channel is the wholesale network Summit already owns, over 3,500 clubs and resorts across 25 countries, where a women's line arriving with a Solheim Cup provenance story gives a buyer a reason to open a new account. B. Draddy sits 62nd globally on the DORMIED Index and was flat month over month, which is roughly what you would expect from a brand whose growth engine is green grass accounts rather than consumer hype.

The real test is 2028 at Valhalla, when the event is domestic, the broadcast window is friendlier and fan merchandise actually moves. If Draddy Sport for Women is a full women's business by then, with its own pro shop footprint and repeat orders, the Solheim deal will look like the cheapest customer acquisition in the category. If it is still a tour-visibility exercise, the throwback scripts will have been the most interesting thing about it.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is new about B. Draddy's role at the 2026 Solheim Cup?

It is the tournament debut of Draddy Sport for Women, the brand's new women's performance line. Zero Restriction, its sister brand under Summit Golf Brands, has supplied U.S. team outerwear at every Solheim Cup since 2013.

Which past teams do the throwback uniforms reference?

Three of the seven scripted days are throwbacks: Monday to 2015, Wednesday to 2007 and Thursday to 1996. Captain Angela Stanford pushed for the historical angle.

Why does Summit Golf Brands' manufacturing setup matter to this deal?

Team scripting is a fulfillment problem as much as a design one. Summit runs more than 200 embroidery heads across over 75,000 square feet in Madison and Barneveld, Wisconsin, which lets it produce hundreds of decorated SKUs against a roster that changes late.

Can fans buy the uniforms?

Yes, at bdraddy.com and zerorestriction.com. The bigger commercial channel is Summit's wholesale network of over 3,500 clubs and resorts in 25 countries, where the Solheim provenance helps open women's accounts.

How long does the uniform agreement run?

B. Draddy and Zero Restriction were named official uniform suppliers to the U.S. Solheim Cup Team in November 2025, covering the 2026 event in the Netherlands and the 2028 edition at Valhalla.
