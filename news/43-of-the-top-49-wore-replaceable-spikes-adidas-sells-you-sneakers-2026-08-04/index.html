43 of the Top 49 Wore Replaceable Spikes. Adidas Sells You Sneakers.

Softspikes says 43 of 49 top finishers wore replaceable spikes. What that streak reveals about adidas, Tour360, Thintech and the spikeless retail shift.

By Adam R.  ·  August 4, 2026  ·  Performance  ·  3 min read

Image: The Golf Wire

What Happened With Adidas Golf?

Softspikes reported 43 of 49 top finishers at the Rocket Classic in replaceable spikes, plus all 30 PGA Tour wins this season. The article argues footwear is the category where tour validation and retail demand have decoupled: adidas defends Tour360 with Thintech while Codechaos, MC80 and Adicross carry volume spikeless.

The number Softspikes wants you to notice is 30. The number worth actually reading is 43 of 49.

Who Won in Softspikes Replaceable Spikes on Tour?

That is how many of the top finishers at the Rocket Classic played in replaceable Softspikes rather than spikeless or tennis-style outsoles, and it arrived in the same release that credited adidas footwear fitted with Thintech spikes for the winner's maiden title, a bogey-free 63 capped by a 26-foot putt at 18 under. Nike footwear with Silver Tornado cleats took the AIG Women's Open at Royal Lytham in a playoff. FootJoy with ProStinger won on PGA Tour Champions. And every week of the 2026 PGA Tour season, all 30 of them, has been won by someone in replaceable traction.

A component supplier claiming a tour streak is like a tire brand claiming a Formula 1 championship: technically accurate, and aimed less at fans than at the people who buy in bulk. Softspikes, manufactured by PrideSports, has been running this playbook since 1993, when a metal spike ban endorsed by several state golf associations handed the company a category to invent. It is the same positioning strategy Golf Pride and KBS use, with one meaningful difference. Shaft and grip companies sell aftermarket to golfers who genuinely care about the spec. Spikes are a $12 clamshell that most amateurs replace roughly never, which is why the release ends with a recommended 15-round replacement cadence. That is the razor-blade line, and it is the actual business.

Why Is Adidas Golf Split Between Tour360 and Codechaos?

What makes this interesting for adidas specifically is the gap it exposes. Tour360, with its low-profile Thintech system, remains a genuine replaceable-spike shoe built for players who need traction in wet Bermuda and torque through impact. Meanwhile the volume side of the adidas footwear business has drifted almost entirely spikeless. Codechaos, MC80, the Adicross line: sneaker-adjacent silhouettes with molded lugs, designed for the golfer who walks nine, gets a beer, and wears the same shoes to dinner. Both products exist for good reasons. But footwear is now the one hardgoods-adjacent category in golf where tour validation and retail demand have fully decoupled. No driver company sells a model that tour pros refuse to play. Every footwear company sells a spikeless line that no one in the final group would consider.

That decoupling matters more than a 30-week streak. It means the tour proof point in footwear has become a legacy asset, useful for defending a shrinking premium tier rather than driving the category. Spikeless won retail years ago on comfort, versatility, and the fact that golf shoes became lifestyle shoes. Replaceable held the tour because elite players are the only cohort willing to trade convenience for four percent more grip on a downhill lie. Softspikes' leaderboard data proves the second point while quietly confirming the first. The company has to keep publishing these numbers precisely because the retail floor stopped listening.

The visibility math for adidas is worth noting too. The brand sits 25th globally in the DORMIED Index this cycle, down 18.3 percent month over month, which is what happens when a brand's category presence in a given month is carried largely by someone else's press release. Thintech got the mention. Softspikes got the headline. Adidas got a footnote in a component supplier's victory lap, which is a strange place for the second-largest apparel and footwear operation in golf to find itself in June.

What Would a Tour360 Outsole Change Mean for Adidas?

Watch the next Tour360 refresh. If adidas keeps Thintech and pushes the performance story harder, it is committing to defend the shrinking replaceable tier where its tour credibility actually lives. If the outsole quietly goes hybrid or molded, that is the signal that even the flagship has been reassigned to the lifestyle side of the business, and the tour shoe becomes a marketing artifact rather than a product line. Softspikes will keep counting weeks either way. The question is how many brands are still building shoes around them when the count reaches 100.
