# What Is Maejer Golf? Mason Mount's New Brand Enters Our Index at 215th of 215

*By Adam R. | Last updated: August 1, 2026*

*Adam R. covers apparel, accessories, and golf culture for DORMIED. He has been tracking brand drops and collaborations since 2018.*

Maejer Golf is a British luxury golf apparel brand launched in late July 2026 by a group that includes Manchester United midfielder Mason Mount, selling polos at $95 and outerwear from $140 to $200 under the tagline "For The Moments." It is also, as of this month, the single least-searched brand in the DORMIED Index: rank 215 of 215, a DI score of 0.0, and zero recorded searches in any of the ten markets we track. That is not an insult. It is a starting line, and it is the reason this piece exists. Almost every brand profile ever written is written after the brand matters. This one is being written in the week it launched, with the data at absolute zero, so that when the September Index lands we will know precisely what a footballer-founded golf brand is worth in actual demand rather than in press releases.

In one sentence: Maejer Golf is a luxury golf apparel brand founded by a collective from fashion and sport, including Mason Mount, that launched its first collection, Chapter 1, in late July 2026 with polos at $95 and jackets up to $200.

Zero is a real measurement, not an absence of one. Maejer has been in our tracking set as a monitored brand and has registered no search demand at all, which is exactly what you would expect from a company whose website went live at the end of July. It is the only brand of the 215 we track in that position. Seven others round down to a DI score of 0.0, but every one of them registers real search volume, between 20 and 110 a month; the DI scale is relative to Titleist's 246,000, so small numbers round to nothing. Maejer is not rounding to zero. It is zero. Every other brand in golf, including labels you have never heard of and several that no longer meaningfully exist, generated more search interest than Maejer did last month.

That changes now, and the interesting question is by how much. Mount's launch announcement went out to a football audience measured in millions. The Index does not measure audience; it measures search, which is what people do when they want something specific enough to type its name. Those are different things, and the gap between them is where most celebrity brands quietly fail. In roughly six weeks we will have the first honest read.

## Who is actually behind Maejer

The brand describes itself as founded by a collective from fashion and sport rather than by a single name, and it has been notably restrained about putting a face on the company. Mount is the most visible founder, and by his own account not a passenger.

Announcing the launch on Instagram, he wrote that Maejer was founded with friends and built from a genuine love of the game, describing it as a brand he had been creatively involved in from day one, inspired by the traditions of golf, the style that surrounds it, and the moments shared on the course. That framing matters commercially. Mount is not being paid to wear this, and he is not an investor bolted onto an existing label. He is a founder, which is a different risk profile and a different story.

The second named name is Owen Farrell, the Saracens fly-half who captained England from 2018 to 2023. Maejer has given him his own post on the company blog, in which Farrell says he is proud to be a partner of Maejer Golf and describes a brand inspired by fashion, tradition and performance that celebrates the moments shared through the game.

The wording there is worth reading closely. Mount says founded with friends and creatively involved from day one. Farrell says partner, and we have created. Those are compatible but not identical claims, and the brand has not published a formal cap table or a full list of who owns what. The rest of the collective, per the company's own account, comes from backgrounds in fashion and sport, and has not been named publicly.

## Mason Mount is a genuine golfer, which is not nothing

The reflexive assumption about any athlete-founded brand is that the athlete is a logo. In Mount's case the record says otherwise, and the detail is more specific than the usual celebrity-loves-golf boilerplate.

Speaking to Dan Rapaport at Pitch Manchester, the city-centre golf venue he is also involved in behind the scenes, Mount described a golf history that starts with his dad taking him to the range at five or six, mostly missing the ball, and then lapsing entirely. What brought him back was watching golf on television during the pandemic, including Netflix's Full Swing, and the addiction took from there. He now plays off roughly 11, or as he put it, "I think a 10.8 or something," and described himself as being at the best scoring level he has reached.

He also tells a story that no non-golfer invents. Playing Archerfield in Scotland, he opened with a double bogey, decided the round was dead, then made a par and went birdie-birdie and could not work out what was happening. He turned defensive, put one out of bounds on the par five, scrambled a par, bogeyed, then went out of bounds again on the next hole and made a triple. That is a real round of golf described by someone who was actually paying attention to it.

He has met Rory McIlroy in Dubai, notes that the tour is full of United supporters including Shane Lowry, the Højgaard brothers, and Aaron Rai, and has never played a pro-am because the invitations, including one to the BMW PGA at Wentworth, land in the middle of the football season. He is a competitive 11-handicap who cannot get out to play as much as he would like, which describes a large share of the market Maejer is selling to.

None of that guarantees the clothes are good. It does mean the brand's claim to be made by golfers, for golfers is defensible rather than decorative.

## What Maejer is actually selling

The first collection is called Chapter 1, and it is small and coherent rather than sprawling, which is the correct way to launch.

Two polos anchor it at $95 each: the Origin Polo, a striped design with ribbed cuffs and contrast tipping that nods to golf's traditional look, and the Mock Neck Polo, a cleaner, minimalist take on the dress code. Outerwear does the heavier lifting and carries most of the design personality. The Flight Bomber at $200 uses anorak-inspired styling with rugby stripes, a detail that reads as a wink at the founders' backgrounds. The Elements Windbreaker at $175 carries an oversized logo across the back. The Condition Jacket sits at $170 and the Monogram Insulated Gilet at $140. Trousers, shorts, and accessories round out the categories, and the whole thing runs on Shopify shipping to around forty markets.

The design language is the current British premium consensus: relaxed fits with room through the chest and shoulders, a slight taper, understated branding, muted palettes of black, stone, taupe, and grey. It sits closest to [Manors](/brands/manors-golf/) in tone, and a long way from the maximalism of [Malbon](/brands/malbon/) or the graphic humour of [Students](/brands/students-golf/).

One observation the brand's own copy invites. Maejer describes itself as luxury golf wear, but a $95 polo is not a luxury price in 2026. It is the same as [Arnie McNair](/brands/arnie-mcnair/) charges for an American-made natural-fibre polo, and well above what [Pins & Aces](/brands/pins-and-aces/) will let anything on its site cost. The pricing is premium-accessible rather than genuinely luxury, which is probably the smarter place to be, and the word luxury is doing marketing work the price tag does not support.

## The gap they say they saw

Maejer's own account of where it came from is more specific than most launch copy. On the company blog, The Cut, the founders describe the brand starting on a golf course during a round that did not matter, in a conversation about why nothing they owned felt right to wear on it.

The detail that follows is the one that undercuts the obvious cynicism. The founders describe the launch shoot, filmed in Scotland, as that same round a few years on, wearing kit they spent a long time getting wrong before they got it right. A few years is not a footballer signing off on a logo between fixtures. Whatever else Maejer is, it has been in development considerably longer than the week it has existed publicly, which is the single most useful thing to know about it right now.

The manifesto page, titled Intention, is blunter still. The founders write that they did not really like many of the golf brands on the market, so they decided to make their own, positioning Maejer as a brand that recognises tradition alongside contemporary design with performance at its heart.

The emotional pitch is more distinctive. The brand states that Maejer was not created to sell you golf but to sell you the feeling of it, arguing that golf is not about the swing, the handicap, or the scorecard but everything around it, the moments people fall in love with but rarely capture. The launch imagery follows the same line: the walk to the first tee, the post-round drink, the conversations that outlast the round.

This is a well-populated position. Selling the feeling rather than the performance is the founding premise of most of the culture-brand wave we cover, from [Random Golf Club](/news/what-is-random-golf-club/) to Students to Malbon. What Maejer has that they did not is a founder with a Premier League platform, and what it lacks is any of their accumulated years of community. Whether the platform substitutes for the community is the actual experiment.

## Why we are writing this now

DORMIED's job is to measure brand demand rather than announce it, and there is a specific reason to plant a flag on a brand with zero search volume.

We have watched this pattern from the other end several times. [Arnie McNair](/news/who-is-arnie-mcnair/) built an audience on anonymous posts before it sold a shirt, and by our May reading was up 205 percent on the quarter. [Pins & Aces](/brands/pins-and-aces/) was not even in our Index at launch until a reader told us we had missed it, and now ranks 29th. In both cases we caught the brand on the way up and had to reconstruct the beginning afterwards.

Maejer gives us the beginning in real time. The brand launched in the final week of July 2026 from a standing start of literally nothing, with a Premier League footballer's audience pointed at it and a product range that is competent, understated, and priced against a crowded field. The September Index will show the first month of real data. Either the launch converts a football audience into golf search demand, which would be a genuinely notable result and a template other athletes will copy, or it produces a spike that decays, which is what usually happens.

We will report whichever it is. That is the entire point of running an index rather than a press office.

## FAQ

**What is Maejer Golf?**
Maejer Golf is a British luxury golf apparel brand that launched its first collection, Chapter 1, in late July 2026. It sells polos at $95, outerwear from $140 to $200, plus trousers, shorts, and accessories, under the tagline "For The Moments."

**Who owns Maejer Golf?**
Maejer describes itself as founded by a collective from fashion and sport. Manchester United midfielder Mason Mount is the most visible founder and has said he was creatively involved from day one. England rugby international Owen Farrell is also involved and describes himself as a partner of the brand. The company has not published a full list of owners or their stakes.

**Is Mason Mount the owner of Maejer Golf?**
He is a co-founder rather than the sole owner. Mount has stated the brand was founded with friends and that he has been creatively involved since the beginning, which distinguishes it from a sponsorship or ambassador arrangement.

**How much does Maejer Golf cost?**
Polos are $95. The Monogram Insulated Gilet is $140, the Condition Jacket $170, the Elements Windbreaker $175, and the Flight Bomber $200.

**Where can you buy Maejer Golf?**
Through the brand's own website, which ships to around forty markets including the United States, United Kingdom, Europe, Canada, Australia, and parts of Asia.

**What does Maejer mean?**
The brand has not publicly explained the origin of the name. It is pronounced and styled as a single word, Maejer, and the company operates as Maejer Golf.

**Is Maejer Golf any good?**
Too early to say on product, and honestly measured on demand it does not yet register. In the June 2026 DORMIED Index, the last full month before launch, Maejer ranked 215th of 215 brands with a DI score of 0.0 and no recorded search interest. The first meaningful read arrives with the September Index.

**What golf handicap is Mason Mount?**
