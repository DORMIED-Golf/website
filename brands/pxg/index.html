PXG

Founded 2014 · Scottsdale, Arizona, USA

Clubs & Balls
#15
Global Rank
View Index →

PXG (Parsons Xtreme Golf) burst onto the golf equipment scene in 2014 with a luxury-first approach that disrupted traditional club pricing and positioning. Founded by billionaire entrepreneur Bob Parsons, who made his fortune selling GoDaddy, the brand built its early reputation on premium materials, aggressive performance claims, and an unapologetic price-is-no-object philosophy. Early PXG irons carried retail prices exceeding $4,000 per set, positioning them as status symbols as much as performance tools. The company's signature aesthetic features bold branding, military-inspired colorways, and design elements like visible screws and tungsten weights that telegraph premium construction. PXG's product line spans drivers, fairway woods, hybrids, irons, wedges, and putters, with models like the 0311 irons and Gen 6 drivers showcasing the brand's focus on multi-material construction and adjustability. The company manufactures its clubs in Scottsdale, emphasizing American production as part of its premium narrative. PXG operates primarily through a direct-to-consumer model, with company-owned fitting studios and an aggressive online presence. The brand has gradually shifted pricing strategy in recent years, introducing more frequent sales and accessible entry points while maintaining its luxury positioning. On tour, PXG has secured endorsements from players including Zach Johnson, Pat Perez, and Joel Dahmen, though its roster remains smaller than major OEMs. Bob Parsons himself remains the brand's most visible spokesperson, appearing in marketing campaigns and social media with a persona that embraces wealth, military veterans (Parsons is a Marine Corps veteran), and an outsider mentality. The brand has drawn both devotees who appreciate the performance and critics who question whether the premium pricing delivers proportional performance gains. PXG represents a segment of one in golf equipment: the luxury disruptor willing to challenge industry conventions on price, marketing, and distribution.
