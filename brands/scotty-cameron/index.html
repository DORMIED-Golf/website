Scotty Cameron

Founded 1991 · San Marcos, California, USA

Clubs & Balls
#7

View Index →

Scotty Cameron has transformed the putter category from a purely functional club choice into an object of desire, collecting, and near-religious devotion among certain golfers. Founded by club designer Don T. "Scotty" Cameron in 1991 and acquired by Titleist (Acushnet Holdings) in 1994, the brand built its reputation on precision-milled putters that combine meticulous craftsmanship with tour validation and carefully cultivated scarcity. Cameron's putters have won hundreds of tour events, including multiple major championships, most notably through Tiger Woods' dominance with various Scotty Cameron models. The brand's core lineup includes the Newport, Squareback, and Fastback designs within the Special Select and Phantom families, featuring multi-material construction with precision-milled faces, aluminum sole plates, and stainless steel bodies. Beyond standard production, Scotty Cameron releases limited-edition and custom shop putters, often in small quantities with intricate paint fills, special materials, and collector-focused details, that command premium prices and immediate sellouts. These limited releases have created a secondary market where certain models trade for multiples of retail price. The Scotty Cameron Studio at Titleist headquarters in Oceanside, California (the brand relocated from original Scottsdale roots) serves as both manufacturing facility and pilgrimage site for devotees. The brand maintains the Scotty Cameron Gallery retail location and offers custom fitting, refinishing services, and limited-edition releases that reinforce the putter-as-luxury-good positioning. The distinctive dancing Cameron logo and headcover designs have become instantly recognizable status symbols. Pricing for standard models starts around $400-500, with limited editions and custom shop creations reaching $1,000 and beyond. Critics question whether the performance justifies the premium, but Scotty Cameron operates in a category where subjective value, feel, aesthetics, brand cachet, matters as much as measurable performance. The brand represents golf equipment at its most collectible and emotionally-driven, where putters function simultaneously as tools and artifacts.
