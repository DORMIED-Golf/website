Key Takeaways

New Level Golf ranks #124 of 215 golf brands on the DORMIED Index for Jun 2026, with a Demand Index score of 1.2, down 19.4% month over month. Its strongest single market is South Korea, where it ranks #108. New Level Golf is a Clubs & Balls brand, founded in 2018, based in Scottsdale, Arizona, USA.

New Level Golf is a Scottsdale-based direct-to-consumer equipment brand launched in 2018, built around the premise that golfers shouldn't have to pay retail markups for tour-quality irons and wedges. The company bypasses traditional distribution channels, selling premium forged clubs directly to consumers through its website and offering extensive custom fitting options without the overhead of brick-and-mortar retail. It's a model borrowed from disruptors in other industries, cutting out the middleman and reinvesting savings into product quality and customer experience. The brand's core offerings are forged irons and wedges designed with input from club designers and tested by skilled players. New Level emphasizes classic shaping, precise milling, and high-grade materials, positioning its products as competitive with legacy brands like Titleist, Mizuno, and TaylorMade but at significantly lower price points. Each club is built to order with custom shaft, grip, length, lie, and loft specifications, allowing players to tailor their sets without paying premium upcharges typical of traditional OEMs. New Level targets serious golfers, typically single-digit handicaps and aspiring competitive players, who understand equipment nuance and are willing to buy online without hands-on retail interaction. The brand has cultivated a loyal following among fitters, instructors, and dedicated amateurs who appreciate its no-frills approach and transparent pricing. While it lacks the tour presence and brand cachet of established equipment giants, New Level has carved out a niche by delivering legitimate performance at accessible price points, particularly appealing in an era when flagship iron sets from major brands routinely exceed two thousand dollars. It's a bet that informed golfers will prioritize substance over logo prestige when given a credible, cost-effective alternative.
