Fujikura

Founded 1994 · Carlsbad, California, USA

Fujikura stands as one of the most technically sophisticated shaft manufacturers in golf, built on the foundation of its parent company's composite materials expertise dating back to 1885. The American golf division, established in 1994 and headquartered in Carlsbad, California, has become the shaft of choice across professional tours worldwide, consistently leading in tour count and validation among elite players. The brand's Ventus line, introduced in 2019, rapidly became the most-played driver shaft on the PGA Tour, engineered with VeloCore technology that stiffens the midsection to reduce ovaling and improve energy transfer. The Speeder franchise remains one of golf's longest-running performance shaft families, continually updated with new composite layups and weight profiles. Fujikura's tour presence is unmatched, walk any professional range and the blue-and-white Fujikura logo dominates, from drivers to fairway woods to hybrids. The company maintains its own tour operations team, custom-building shafts for players and collecting real-world performance data that feeds back into R&D. Beyond tour-level products, Fujikura manufactures OEM shafts for major equipment brands and offers extensive fitting options through authorized dealers and custom builders. The brand positions itself at the premium end of the aftermarket shaft category, with most models priced between $350 and $500, reflecting the advanced materials science and manufacturing precision involved. In recent years, Fujikura expanded into iron shafts with models like the Ventus TR, applying driver shaft technology to the iron category. The company's reputation rests on consistency, performance validation at the highest competitive levels, and a relentless focus on measurable improvements in ball flight and feel, qualities that matter most when the only variable between success and failure is the stick connecting hands to clubhead.

THE READ Mar 2026

Ventus shafts are still showing up in the bags of McIlroy and Burns, which is exactly where a premium shaft brand wants to be seen, and the nearly 50% three-month surge suggests the tour visibility is translating to consumer curiosity. Sitting at 54th is respectable but unremarkable for a brand that peaked at 31 less than a year ago. The momentum is real, but Fujikura needs to sustain it through the summer fitting season or risk settling back into aftermarket anonymity.
