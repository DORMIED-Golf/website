Key Takeaways

Public Drip ranks #166 of 215 golf brands on the DORMIED Index for Jul 2026, with a Demand Index score of 0.5, up +47.7% month over month. Its strongest single market is Germany, where it ranks #119. Public Drip is an Apparel & Footwear brand, founded in 2020, based in Los Angeles, California, USA.

Public Drip launched in 2020 from Los Angeles as a golf lifestyle brand designed for the social media generation. Founded during golf's pandemic-era participation boom, the brand capitalizes on the game's cultural shift toward younger, more diverse players who view golf as a social activity rather than a competitive pursuit. Public Drip's apparel and accessories blend streetwear aesthetics with golf functionality, featuring bold graphics, relaxed fits, and designs that reference golf culture through a contemporary lens. The brand name itself signals its target audience, golfers who play public courses and prioritize style and community over country club tradition. Public Drip built its business entirely through social media marketing and influencer partnerships, creating a community-driven brand identity that emphasizes golf as lifestyle rather than sport. Its product range includes hoodies, T-shirts, hats, and accessories that work as well off the course as on it, appealing to golfers who want to signal their interest in the game without wearing traditional golf apparel. The brand's marketing leans heavily into golf's social aspects, post-round drinks, group outings, and the culture surrounding the game, rather than performance or improvement. Public Drip represents a category of golf brands emerging from the intersection of streetwear, social media, and golf's accessibility movement. While traditionalists might dismiss it as fashion over function, the brand resonates with new golfers who discovered the game through social channels and want apparel that reflects their broader style preferences. Public Drip's success depends on sustaining community engagement and cultural relevance as golf's pandemic boost normalizes, but it effectively captures a market segment that legacy brands struggled to reach.
