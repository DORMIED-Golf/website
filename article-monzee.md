# What Is Monzee? The Japanese Golf Brand Named After a Made-Up Monkey

*By Adam R. | Last updated: September 8, 2026*

*Adam R. covers apparel, accessories, and golf culture for DORMIED. He has been tracking brand drops and collaborations since 2018.*

Monzee is a Japanese golf brand best known for headcovers, bags and accessories, and it is named after an animal that does not exist. By the brand's own account, monzee is an imaginary creature born from a mix of monkey and chimpanzee, chosen because monkeys move swiftly through the trees and golf, played properly, is a matter of navigating and surviving a forest. It is a strange origin story for a golf company, and it is the most honest signal of what the brand is: a design project first.

In one sentence: Monzee is a Japanese golf accessories brand founded by a designer who grew up in Carlsbad, California, making headcovers and bags with a distinct point of view, and it has collaborated with Engineered Garments on a capsule built from upcycled apparel and fabric.

## A Japanese brand with a Southern California childhood

The origin detail is the part most people miss. Monzee is Japanese, but its design sensibility comes from Carlsbad, in Southern California, where its designer, Yuki, grew up. Carlsbad is not a random American suburb. It is one of the densest concentrations of golf equipment manufacturing anywhere in the world, and growing up there means growing up inside the industry rather than adjacent to it.

Yuki's stated ambition is narrow and unusual for the category: essential golf items with a distinct point of view, built from a perspective formed around golf and music from an early age. The brand notes, for what it is worth, that his personal best is 62 and he is still trying to improve on it.

That inversion is worth sitting with. Japanese production applied to an American upbringing, in a product category that mostly exists to stop a driver getting scratched.

It also puts Monzee in a specific lineage. Japan has a long history of studying American clothing more carefully than Americans do, then remaking it better: denim, workwear, Ivy style, military surplus. Monzee is that tradition arriving at golf accessories.

## The Engineered Garments collaboration

The moment that best explains Monzee's standing is its capsule with Engineered Garments.

Engineered Garments is one of the most respected names in Japanese-American menswear, a label with deep credibility among people who care about construction and fabric and no obvious reason to work with a golf brand. The two came together on a collection of golf club covers and bags, applying what Engineered Garments described as a shared brand ethos of alternative perspectives, upcycling unused apparel and fabric into a capsule collection. The pieces were built from jackets and pant pocket panels.

That is a meaningful endorsement, and it is also the clearest statement of method. Taking a finished garment apart and rebuilding it as a headcover means every piece is constrained by whatever material was on hand, which is the opposite of how golf accessories are normally produced: a design finalised, then made in volume for as long as it sells.

Engineered Garments does not need golf, and a golf accessory brand does not normally get access to that room. Monzee did, because both companies are doing the same thing: taking existing material seriously and building something considered out of it.

## Why it matters

DORMIED has written before about the headcover market and why certain covers hold value: scarcity, craft, provenance, story. Monzee is what happens when a brand starts from those principles rather than backing into them.

There is no obvious volume play here and no apparent attempt at one. A small catalogue, a designer with a specific point of view, and a collaborator list that reads like a menswear label's rather than a golf brand's. Whether that ever becomes a large business is doubtful, and probably beside the point. What it demonstrates is that golf accessories can be treated as objects worth making properly, and that when someone does, an audience exists well outside golf.

## FAQ

**What is Monzee?**
Monzee is a Japanese golf brand making headcovers, bags and accessories, with a design point of view drawn from its designer's Southern California upbringing.

**What does the name Monzee mean?**
The brand says monzee is an imaginary creature made from a mix of monkey and chimpanzee, referencing the way monkeys move through trees and the idea of golf as navigating a forest.

**Where is Monzee from?**
Japan. The brand sells through monzee.jp. Its design sensibility, however, comes from Carlsbad, in Southern California.

**Who designs Monzee?**
A designer named Yuki, who grew up in Carlsbad surrounded by golf and music. The brand does not publish a surname.

**What does Monzee make?**
Primarily golf club headcovers, bags and accessories, alongside a smaller apparel range.

**Has Monzee collaborated with other brands?**
Yes. Most notably with Engineered Garments, on a capsule collection of golf club covers and bags built by upcycling unused apparel and fabric, including jackets and pant pocket panels.

**Where can you buy Monzee?**
Through the brand's own site, monzee.jp, which ships its headcovers, bags and apparel.
